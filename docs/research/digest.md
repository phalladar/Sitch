## Summary
The original Skitch for Mac (plasq 2007 beta, acquired by Evernote August 18, 2011) defined the snap-annotate-drag-share genre: crosshair capture on Shift-Cmd-5, an iconic pink arrow, a vertical toolbar of ~7 simple tools with an 8-color fixed palette, drag-the-window-corner image resizing, drag-the-image-edge cropping, and the famous "Drag Me" tab protruding from the bottom edge for drag-out sharing. Evernote's 2012 Skitch 2.0 rewrite stripped these conventions (1.5-star backlash forced partial restoration), then Evernote killed Skitch on Windows/iOS/Android in January 2016 and left the Mac app to rot after v2.9 (2020): Intel-only, Rosetta-dependent on Apple Silicon, crashing on launch on the newest macOS. A faithful clone must keep the pink tapered arrow, drag-me tab with format selector, window-edge resize/crop, small fixed palette, five stamps (check/X/?/!/heart), editable vector annotations, one-click share URL with clipboard copy, menu-bar residency with global hotkey, and capture history — while avoiding the account/cloud lock-in that killed 2.0's reputation.

## Facts
- Skitch originated as a product of the Australian studio plasq (makers of Comic Life); public beta posts date to May 2007 (andypiper.co.uk May 18, 2007; cdevroe.com May 21, 2007), and it ran as an invite-only beta through at least October 2007.
- Evernote acquired Skitch on August 18, 2011 (per iskitch.com/blog: 'Evernote acquired Skitch on 18 August 2011').
- Signature arrow: MacStories describes 'the typical pink arrow that you can drag around and modify'; screensnap.pro calls them 'the iconic pink arrows' and 'Skitch's famous pink arrow, great for look-here moments'; App Store description says arrows are resizable and rotatable in 45-degree increments.
- The 'Drag Me' tab (PCWorld, verbatim): 'a tab that sticks out the bottom of the window, protruding outside the border. The tab just says Drag Me, and when you comply, you get a local copy of the file' — draggable into an instant message, email, the desktop, or a document at any point mid-edit.
- In later versions the Drag Me tab doubled as the file-type selector ('the Drag Me tab as a feature for easily selecting the file type for your image' — screensnap.pro), and per a user review 'the skitch window fades away as you drag to make it easy to put the image where you want.'
- Resize UX: 'Simply resizing Skitch to be the size you want your screenshot to be will resize your screenshot automatically' — dragging the window corners physically resizes the image inside it, with the pixel size 'indicated in the bottom left hand corner' (cdevroe.com, andypiper.co.uk).
- Crop UX: crop/expand canvas by grabbing 'edges of the image inside the window' and dragging from a side or corner (andypiper.co.uk; disruptiveconversations.com: 'go to one of the sides or corners of the image and start dragging to crop').
- Toolbar (PCWorld, verbatim): a vertical toolbar with seven tools — 'an arrow for pointing things out, a text tool, a color picker with a limited palette of just eight colors, a rectangle you can surround objects with, a highlighter, a pixelizer for blurring out details, and a crop tool'; colors sit at the bottom of the toolbar; a 'size slider on the left of the window' set text/line thickness (andypiper.co.uk).
- Final Mac tool set (App Store v2.9 + Evernote help): arrows; text with adjustable wrapping; rectangles, rounded rectangles, ellipses, lines; freehand pen; yellow marker-style transparent-ink highlighter; pixelate for sensitive info; stamps; crop and canvas expansion.
- Stamps (added in the April 18, 2013 update, per SlashGear): exactly five — green checkmark (approval), red X (needs changes), exclamation mark (needs attention), question mark (confusing), heart (excellent, don't change); stamps can carry short text notes; the same update added PDF annotation as an Evernote Premium feature with a 30-day free trial.
- Capture modes: Crosshair Snapshot (area selection) on Shift-Cmd-5 (a hotkey that conflicts with macOS Mojave's built-in screenshot UI, firing both), window snap, full-screen snap, timed screen snap, and opening existing images/PDFs; in Skitch 1.x you could 'hold down the Shift key while taking a screen shot to get a countdown timer' for capturing menus and hover states.
- Sharing in 1.x: a 'webpost' button in the lower-right corner uploaded to a personal mySkitch page with a simple URL; Skitch 1.x also supported .Mac, FTP, SFTP, WebDAV, and Flickr, plus auto-created share URLs, shortened URLs, and automatic copy-link-to-clipboard.
- History UX: 'a quick click on the history button in the upper right hand corner of the window flips you over to a grid based view of all your previous Skitches'; Skitch kept a local copy of all creations, and its delete function remembered where a file was shared, offering to delete the local file, the shared remote copy, or both (cdevroe.com).
- Skitch 1.x window quirks: no standard document chrome in the beta — 'Preferences on the back of the window, more like a Dashboard widget'; minimize by double-clicking the title bar; annotations were layer-based vector objects that could be moved and edited later; export formats were JPG, PNG, SVG, PDF, or the layered .skitch format; the app could run from the menu bar with custom hotkeys.
- Skitch 2.0 (September 20, 2012) backlash: the rewrite removed drag-corner resize, drag-edge crop, the format dropdown (JPG/PNG/TIF/PDF — 2.0 exported only PNG), timed screenshots, the menu bar icon, custom/global hotkeys, background operation, FTP/SFTP/WebDAV/Flickr, auto URL creation, and auto clipboard copy, and made Evernote the only storage; it hit a 1.5-star Mac App Store rating with almost 600 one-star reviews.
- Evernote's response to the 2.0 backlash: v2.0.1 (September 28, 2012) restored some features and Skitch 1.0 was made downloadable again; a December 2012 update restored more 'classic' features; the published roadmap promised FTP/sFTP uploads, auto sharing URLs, short URLs, direct image hosting, multiple fonts and custom colors, streamlined cropping and resizing, and automatic type-tool selection.
- Death, phase 1: Evernote announced on December 17, 2015 that Skitch for Windows, Windows Touch, iOS, and Android would be discontinued effective January 22, 2016, keeping only the Mac version; official line: 'As part of our promise to focus on improving the core Evernote experience, we are ending support for several standalone apps'; Clearly and the Pebble app were killed simultaneously (TechCrunch).
- Death, phase 2: the Mac app's last release was version 2.9 — dated 07/07/2020 on the Mac App Store (25.6 MB, macOS 10.11+, seller 'Skitch Inc', free with in-app purchases) and April 20, 2020 on MacUpdate (30.4 MB, rating 2.6/5 from 139 users); Evernote also removed Evernote sync from Skitch itself.
- Apple Silicon breakage: the binary is Intel 64 only with 'no native Apple Silicon build, so it leans on Rosetta and degrades over time'; reported failures include 'crashes on launch on the newest macOS releases', 'broken text annotations after certain macOS updates', 'NOT compatible with macOS Tahoe, crashing on startup' (MacUpdate user review), dead Twitter/Facebook share buttons, and screenshots capturing only the desktop unless Screen Recording permission is manually granted.
- Why it was beloved: 'capture something, slap an arrow and some text on it, and share it in seconds' (iskitch.com); 'Skitch goes out of its way to keep things simple and coherent' with no special effects or fancy filters (PCWorld); it removed the need for Photoshop, which was 'far too complex for this type of simple annotation' (cdevroe.com); Hack Design says it 'peaked around 2013-2016 when screen annotation tools were rare' with the draw being 'take a screenshot, annotate in seconds, and share via drag-and-drop or email'.
- Why Evernote abandoned it (screensnap.pro analysis): 'Evernote wanted users in their main app, not in standalone tools. They even removed Evernote sync from Skitch, with the message clear — use Evernote, not this better tool we bought'; Evernote claimed it had 'incorporated many of the best features of Skitch and Clearly in our core applications'.
- Late-Skitch feature losses documented by screensnap.pro: 'You can no longer use the eraser to break a square into two parts', SVG export was removed, Evernote sync was eliminated, and Skitch never gained blur (only pixelate).
- Skitch 3 for iOS (2014 rebrand, MacStories): new logo of 'an arrow with feathers'; editing tools and colors lived in 'two separate circles at the bottom of the screen' that expand 'with a bouncy animation'; annotations remained draggable/resizable objects with color switching after placement.
- iSkitch (the modern remake, iskitch.com): 'a modern, native macOS take on the classic Skitch'; region capture on Option-Cmd-4 plus window and full-screen modes opening 'straight in the editor'; 14 tools — Select, Arrow (tapered), Text, Rectangle, Ellipse, Line, Highlight, Pixelate, Stamp, Pen, Spotlight, Sticker, Image, Crop; $9.99 one-time purchase, no subscriptions or accounts; macOS 14+, Apple Silicon and Intel; 'Native SwiftUI + AppKit. Lives in the menu bar.'
- App tagline throughout the Evernote era: 'Skitch — Snap. Mark up. Share.' with the App Store pitch 'snap it, mark it up, and send it on in an instant.'

## Recommendations
- Keep the pink tapered arrow as the default, pre-selected tool: drawn by click-dragging from tail toward target so it 'points and stretches in the direction you drag', and it must remain a movable, resizable, rotatable object after placement.
- Keep the 'Drag Me' tab protruding from the bottom edge of the editor window: dragging it exports the flattened image into any drop target (Slack, email, Finder, browser); pair it with an adjacent file-format selector (PNG/JPG/PDF/TIFF) and fade the window during the drag.
- Keep the snap-first loop: global hotkey -> crosshair region selection (plus window and full-screen modes, and a Shift-held countdown timer for menus/hover states) -> capture lands directly in the editor ready to annotate, with no intermediate dialog.
- Keep direct-manipulation resize and crop: dragging the window corner resizes the image itself (live pixel dimensions shown in the bottom-left), and dragging the image's edges inside the window crops or expands the canvas — no Crop/Resize dialog (removing this was 2.0's most-hated change).
- Keep the small fixed palette (~8 swatches, pink featured) at the bottom of a vertical left-edge toolbar of at most 7-8 tools (arrow, text, shape, highlighter, pixelate, pen, stamp, crop) with a single size slider — resist adding a full color picker or tool sprawl as the primary UI.
- Keep the five classic stamps: green checkmark, red X, question mark, exclamation mark, heart — with optional short text attached to each stamp.
- Keep annotations as editable vector layers until export, auto-switch to the text tool when the user starts typing, and provide a history grid of past captures reachable from a button in the window's upper-right.
- Keep frictionless sharing: one click produces a hosted share URL that is automatically copied to the clipboard (short URL), alongside local save, clipboard copy, and drag-out — never make a cloud account the only storage path.
- Keep menu-bar residency, customizable global hotkeys, and background operation (no forced Dock presence); ship native Apple Silicon + Intel builds and request Screen Recording permission properly.
- Avoid the mistakes that killed Skitch's reputation: no account lock-in, no subscription requirement for core markup, no removal of format choice, and no dependency on a parent service's roadmap — the 2012 Skitch 2.0 backlash (1.5 stars, ~600 one-star reviews) is the canonical cautionary tale.

## Open questions
- The exact hex value of Skitch's signature pink is not documented in any accessible source; it would need to be sampled from a screenshot of the original app or its assets.
- The exact composition of the eight-color palette (likely pink, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, white, black) is asserted as 'eight colors' by PCWorld but never enumerated in any source found.
- Version 2.9's release date conflicts across sources: Mac App Store says 07/07/2020, MacUpdate says April 20, 2020, and screensnap.pro claims the last update was 2016 — the 2016 date likely refers to the last feature update vs. 2020 maintenance release.
- The Gear Live 2007 review (potentially rich in 1.x palette/toolbar detail) returned HTTP 403 and web.archive.org is unreachable from this environment, so its contents could not be verified.

# TOPIC: capture-apis

## Summary
For a Skitch-like .NET screenshot app on Windows there are three capture backends. (1) GDI BitBlt with CAPTUREBLT: works on every Windows version, dead simple, one-shot friendly, captures the composited desktop at physical pixels (if the process is per-monitor-DPI aware), but clamps HDR content to washed-out SDR and cannot capture occluded/minimized windows individually — this is what ShareX and Greenshot actually ship for stills. (2) DXGI Desktop Duplication (Windows 8+): per-monitor GPU frame streaming, DXGI_FORMAT_B8G8R8A8_UNORM by default, HDR via IDXGIOutput5::DuplicateOutput1 with R16G16B16A16_FLOAT (Win10 1703+); it's monitor-only (no per-window), requires rotation and cursor handling in code, and has adapter/session/RDP quirks — best for video recording, overkill for stills. (3) Windows.Graphics.Capture (WinRT, namespace since Win10 1803, HWND/HMONITOR interop via IGraphicsCaptureItemInterop since 1903): captures a specific window even when occluded (no shadow, title bar included), per-monitor capture, selectable pixel format including FP16 for HDR, cursor toggle (SDK 19041), but on Windows 10 always draws a yellow capture border (removable only on build 20348+/Win11 via GraphicsCaptureAccess.RequestAccessAsync(Borderless) + IsBorderRequired=false), and it's a frame-pool streaming API so a "screenshot" means start session, take first frame, stop. Region select across mixed-DPI monitors is best done with the freeze-frame pattern (capture entire virtual screen once, then show borderless topmost overlay window(s) — ideally one per monitor — over it) with the process declared PerMonitorV2 so all Win32 coordinates are physical pixels. Window picking = EnumWindows filtered by IsWindowVisible + DWMWA_CLOAKED (UWP ApplicationFrameWindow hosts a Windows.UI.Core.CoreWindow child) + WS_EX_TOOLWINDOW, with rectangles from DWMWA_EXTENDED_FRAME_BOUNDS (excludes the DWM shadow; GetWindowRect includes shadow/invisible borders). PrintWindow with undocumented flag PW_RENDERFULLCONTENT (0x2, Win8.1+) captures DirectComposition/UWP content of occluded windows. ShareX = BitBlt(SRCCOPY|CAPTUREBLT) + frozen-canvas region form + DWM extended-frame-bounds window rects (with a long tail of mixed-DPI bugs); Greenshot = PrintWindow for GDI mode and a DwmRegisterThumbnail-onto-temp-form trick, capturing over white and black backgrounds to reconstruct per-pixel alpha (shadow/transparency); Windows 11 Snipping Tool = packaged Microsoft.ScreenSketch app whose Win+Shift+S overlay is hosted by ScreenClippingHost.exe (MicrosoftWindows.Client.CBS SystemApp), with an "HDR Screenshot Color Corrector" toggle added 2024. Recommended .NET stack: target net8.0-windows10.0.19041.0+, PMv2 manifest, freeze-frame overlay per monitor, WGC (via interop + Direct3D11CaptureFramePool) as primary window/monitor backend, BitBlt+CAPTUREBLT as universal region/fallback backend, PrintWindow+PW_RENDERFULLCONTENT as occluded-window fallback, SetWindowDisplayAffinity(WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE) to hide any live overlay from capture.

## Facts
- Windows.Graphics.Capture (WGC): GraphicsCaptureSession class requires 'Windows 10, version 1803 (introduced in 10.0.17134.0)', API contract Windows.Foundation.UniversalApiContract v6.0 (learn.microsoft.com GraphicsCaptureSession page).
- WGC Win32 interop: IGraphicsCaptureItemInterop::CreateForWindow(HWND) and ::CreateForMonitor(HMONITOR) 'allow the graphics capture API to target a single window or monitor given its ntuser handles'; introduced for the Windows 10 May 2019 Update (version 1903) per Microsoft Learn and the Windows Developer Blog post 'New Ways to do Screen Capture' (Sept 16, 2019).
- WGC capture flow: D3D11CreateDevice -> CreateDirect3D11DeviceFromDXGIDevice -> Direct3D11CaptureFramePool.Create/CreateFreeThreaded(device, pixelFormat, numberOfBuffers, size) -> framePool.CreateCaptureSession(item) -> session.StartCapture() -> FrameArrived yields Direct3D11CaptureFrame whose .Surface is convertible to ID3D11Texture2D (then CopyResource to a staging texture and Map to read pixels). Reference implementation: github.com/robmikh/Win32CaptureSample and microsoft/Windows.UI.Composition-Win32-Samples (WPF ScreenCapture sample, CaptureHelper.cs).
- WGC is a streaming API — for a still screenshot you start a session, take the first frame, then Close(); Direct3D11CaptureFramePool.Recreate must be called when the captured item's ContentSize changes.
- WGC on Windows 10 always draws a yellow border around captured content (OBS forum thread 'Windows Graphics Capture vs DXGI Desktop Duplication' describes it as a Windows 10 limitation); the border only became removable starting build 20348.
- GraphicsCaptureSession.IsBorderRequired: 'Gets or sets a value specifying whether the capture operation requires a colored border around the window or display to indicate that a capture is in progress' — Device family 'Windows 10, version 2104 (introduced in 10.0.20348.0)', UniversalApiContract v12.0. Docs: 'Before the system will disable the colored border... your app must get consent from the user by calling GraphicsCaptureAccess.RequestAccessAsync, passing in the value GraphicsCaptureAccessKind.Borderless'. 'If the user denies access, setting this property to false will succeed, but the value will be ignored'. Packaged apps 'must declare the graphicsCaptureWithoutBorder capability' in the package manifest.
- GraphicsCaptureAccess class ('Allows the app to request access for graphics capture features that require user consent', method RequestAccessAsync(GraphicsCaptureAccessKind)) also introduced in 10.0.20348.0, UniversalApiContract v12.0.
- GraphicsCaptureSession version history on Microsoft Learn lists IsCursorCaptureEnabled added in Windows version 2004 / SDK 19041 ('Gets or sets a value specifying whether the capture session will include the cursor in the captured content').
- Newer GraphicsCaptureSession properties visible only under the winrt-26100 (Windows 11 24H2) and later doc monikers: DirtyRegionMode, MinUpdateInterval, ConfigurationIteration, and IncludeSecondaryWindows ('Gets or sets a value indicating whether secondary windows are included in the capture session' — i.e., popups/tooltips owned by the captured window, which classic WGC window capture omits).
- WGC window capture captures the window's composited content (title bar + client area, no DWM drop shadow) even when the window is occluded by other windows, but cannot capture minimized windows; GraphicsCaptureSession.IsSupported() reports device support.
- WGC HDR: the frame pool pixel format is caller-selectable (DirectXPixelFormat); use R16G16B16A16Float to receive HDR/WCG scRGB content and B8G8R8A8UIntNormalized for SDR; capturing an HDR desktop into 8-bit BGRA without tone mapping produces the well-known washed-out result.
- DXGI Desktop Duplication (DDA): introduced in Windows 8 ('Windows 8 disables standard XDDM mirror drivers and offers the desktop duplication API instead' — MS Learn 'Desktop Duplication API'); per-output duplication via IDXGIOutput1::DuplicateOutput; 'The format of the desktop image is always DXGI_FORMAT_B8G8R8A8_UNORM no matter what the current display mode is'.
- DDA frame acquisition: IDXGIOutputDuplication::AcquireNextFrame(timeout) returns DXGI_ERROR_WAIT_TIMEOUT when nothing changed and DXGI_ERROR_ACCESS_LOST on desktop switch/mode change (must re-create duplication); metadata via GetFrameDirtyRects and GetFrameMoveRects; 'your client app must first process all the move regions and then process all the dirty regions'.
- DDA rotation: 'In a rotated mode, the surface that you receive from AcquireNextFrame is always in the un-rotated orientation, and the desktop image is rotated within the surface' — the client must rotate the image itself; each monitor rotates independently.
- DDA mouse pointer: the pointer may be delivered separately from the desktop image (DXGI_OUTDUPL_FRAME_INFO.PointerPosition + IDXGIOutputDuplication::GetFramePointerShape) and 'your client app must draw the mouse pointer shape onto the desktop image' when a separate pointer is reported.
- DDA constraints: 'Requires the application to run on the same GPU as the display' / requires a monitor plugged into the capturing adapter (fails with errors like DXGI_ERROR_UNSUPPORTED on hybrid-GPU laptops when created on the wrong adapter); DDA is monitor-granular only — it cannot capture an individual window (OBS forum + robmikh Win32CaptureSample issue #24 discussion).
- DDA HDR: IDXGIOutput5::DuplicateOutput1 (dxgi1_5.h) 'allows specifying a list of supported formats for fullscreen surfaces', enabling capture of HDR/WCG content e.g. DXGI_FORMAT_R16G16B16A16_FLOAT (default scRGB color space DXGI_COLOR_SPACE_RGB_FULL_G10_NONE_P709, same as DWM); classic DuplicateOutput 'always converts to 32-bit BGRA'. Windows 10 version 1703 introduced the first version of Advanced Color/HDR capabilities (MS Learn 'Use DirectX with Advanced Color' + DuplicateOutput1 page).
- GDI BitBlt: works on Windows XP+ everywhere; capture = GetWindowDC(NULL or hwnd) + CreateCompatibleDC + BitBlt with SRCCOPY | CAPTUREBLT (CAPTUREBLT = 0x40000000, needed to include layered WS_EX_LAYERED windows); ShareX's Screenshot.CaptureRectangleNative does exactly 'BitBlt(hdcDest, 0, 0, rect.Width, rect.Height, hdcSrc, rect.X, rect.Y, CopyPixelOperation.SourceCopy | CopyPixelOperation.CaptureBlt)' (ShareX.ScreenCaptureLib/Screenshot.cs).
- GDI BitBlt limitations: no cursor in the capture (must composite manually via GetCursorInfo + GetIconInfo hotspot + DrawIconEx); HDR desktops come out washed-out/clamped SDR (numerous MS Q&A threads, e.g. 'All Screenshots come out washed'); relatively CPU-heavy; if the process is not DPI-aware, virtualized GetSystemMetrics/coordinates cause scaled or cropped captures.
- PrintWindow(hwnd, hdc, flags): documented flags are only PW_CLIENTONLY ('Only the client area of the window is copied to hdcBlt. By default, the entire window is copied.'); it works by sending WM_PRINT/WM_PRINTCLIENT so 'The application that owns the window... renders the image', which is why it can capture occluded (but not minimized) windows; min OS Windows XP (MS Learn PrintWindow page).
- PW_RENDERFULLCONTENT = 0x00000002 exists in winuser.h since Windows 8.1 but is NOT documented on the PrintWindow page; it asks DWM to render the window's full composited content, making PrintWindow work for DirectComposition/UWP/Chromium windows that come out black with flags=0 (used by OBS and Chromium; corroborated by fengyuan.com WM_PRINT article and community docs).
- Window rectangles: on Windows 10/11 GetWindowRect includes the invisible resize borders / drop-shadow area ('Windows 10 has a subtle drop shadow around three edges of a window... classed as being part of the window' — Cyotek 'Getting a window rectangle without the drop shadow'); DwmGetWindowAttribute(DWMWA_EXTENDED_FRAME_BOUNDS) 'Retrieves the extended frame bounds rectangle in screen space' and returns the visible bounds WITHOUT the shadow.
- DPI trap: DWMWA_EXTENDED_FRAME_BOUNDS is not DPI-virtualized — it returns physical pixels even when the calling process is not per-monitor aware, while GetWindowRect returns virtualized coordinates; the two disagree unless the process runs PerMonitorV2 (ben.stolovitz.com 'Resize a WinUI window' + community reports). Run the app PMv2 so every screen coordinate is physical and consistent.
- Cloaked windows: DwmGetWindowAttribute(DWMWA_CLOAKED) returns DWM_CLOAKED_APP (0x0000001, 'cloaked by its owner application'), DWM_CLOAKED_SHELL (0x0000002, 'cloaked by the Shell' — e.g. suspended UWP apps or windows on another virtual desktop), DWM_CLOAKED_INHERITED (0x0000004, 'inherited from its owner window'); requires Windows 8+ (MS Learn DWMWINDOWATTRIBUTE). IsWindowVisible still returns TRUE for cloaked UWP windows, so an alt-tab-style picker must combine IsWindowVisible + not-cloaked + GetWindowTextLength>0 + skip WS_EX_TOOLWINDOW (Raymond Chen 'How can I detect that my window has been suppressed from the screen by the shell?'; OBS libobs/util/windows/window-helpers.c is a battle-tested filter implementation).
- UWP window structure: a UWP app's top-level window has class name 'ApplicationFrameWindow' hosting a child of class 'Windows.UI.Core.CoreWindow'; Greenshot detects them via 'public bool IsWin10App => AppFrameWindowClass.Equals(ClassName)' and flags background (suspended) UWP apps as ApplicationFrameWindow WITHOUT a CoreWindow child (WindowDetails.cs, greenshot/greenshot release/1.3, src/Greenshot.Base/Core/WindowDetails.cs).
- Capture-a-window-without-shadow recipe: BitBlt/WGC the screen region returned by DWMWA_EXTENDED_FRAME_BOUNDS (this is what Alt+PrintScreen effectively produces — it never includes the drop shadow); WGC CreateForWindow and PrintWindow also exclude the shadow because the shadow is drawn by DWM outside the window.
- Capture-a-window-WITH-shadow/transparency recipe (Greenshot 'AeroTransparent' mode, CaptureDwmWindow in WindowDetails.cs): DwmApi.DwmRegisterThumbnail(tempForm.Handle, Handle, out thumbnailHandle) renders the live window onto a temporary borderless form placed at the window's location; the form's BackColor is set to Color.White, screen-captured, then set to Color.Black and captured again, and ApplyTransparency(blackBitmap, whiteBitmap) reconstructs the per-pixel alpha channel (including shadow and translucent borders) from the two captures; cleanup via DwmApi.DwmUnregisterThumbnail. Non-transparent DWM mode fills the backdrop with Conf.DWMBackgroundColor or DwmApi.ColorizationColor instead.
- Greenshot's GDI path (CaptureGdiWindow) simply calls PrintWindow(); Greenshot's configured modes are Screen / GDI / Aero / AeroTransparent / Auto (CoreConfiguration WindowCaptureMode); on Windows 10 it applies 'Conf.Win10BorderCrop' via windowRect.Inflate() to trim a 1px border artifact from extended frame bounds, and its visibility check is 'public bool Visible => IsCloaked ? false : User32Api.IsWindowVisible(Handle)'.
- ShareX full-screen/region capture is pure GDI: CaptureFullscreen() -> CaptureHelpers.GetScreenBounds() -> CaptureRectangleNative (BitBlt SRCCOPY|CAPTUREBLT), with a managed Graphics.CopyFromScreen fallback (CaptureRectangleManaged) kept 'to avoid .NET bugs'; window capture takes the window's screen rectangle (CaptureHelpers.GetWindowRectangle, which prefers DWM extended frame bounds when DWM is enabled) and crops the desktop BitBlt — no PrintWindow — so ShareX cannot capture occluded windows; ShareX also has AutoHideTaskbar via NativeMethods.SetTaskbarVisibilityIfIntersect(false, rect).
- ShareX region-select overlay (RegionCaptureForm.cs) is the freeze-frame pattern with ONE window spanning all monitors: 'if (canvas == null) { canvas = new Screenshot().CaptureRectangle(ScreenBounds); }' displayed as a TextureBrush background in a form with 'FormBorderStyle = FormBorderStyle.None; Bounds = ScreenBounds; ShowInTaskbar = false;', TopMost=true, activated via ForceActivate(), custom crosshair cursor (Helpers.CreateCursor(Resources.Crosshair)), and monitor selection via number keys (RegionResult.Monitor).
- ShareX's single spanning form + mixed per-monitor DPI has produced a long trail of bugs: GitHub issues #165, #1477, #3578 ('Add support for multi-monitor setup with different DPI settings'), #4073, #5312 ('ShareX does not support high DPI monitors'), #5412 (cursor offset in recordings with 100%+125% mix), #7713, #8002 (capture offset), #8238 (recording overlay clipped off main monitor) — evidence that a mixed-DPI overlay needs per-monitor windows and full PMv2 awareness. ShareX's current master branch is an Avalonia rewrite whose app.manifest contains no dpiAware/dpiAwareness element at all.
- Per-monitor-DPI overlay guidance (MS Learn 'High DPI Desktop Application Development on Windows'): DPI_AWARENESS_CONTEXT_PER_MONITOR_AWARE_V2 (PMv2) is supported on Windows 10 1703+; under PMv2 'the application sees the raw pixels of each display' and 'is never bitmap scaled by Windows', top-level windows get WM_DPICHANGED when crossing monitors; declare it in the app manifest (<dpiAwareness>PerMonitorV2</dpiAwareness>) or call SetProcessDpiAwarenessContext before any HWND is created; in WinForms use Application.SetHighDpiMode(HighDpiMode.PerMonitorV2) (.NET Core 3.0+).
- Multi-monitor geometry: the virtual screen is addressed with GetSystemMetrics(SM_XVIRTUALSCREEN=76, SM_YVIRTUALSCREEN=77, SM_CXVIRTUALSCREEN=78, SM_CYVIRTUALSCREEN=79) — left/top can be negative because the primary monitor always contains (0,0); enumerate monitors with EnumDisplayMonitors + GetMonitorInfo(MONITORINFOEXW) (DesktopCoordinates in physical px under PMv2), per-monitor scale via GetDpiForMonitor(hmon, MDT_EFFECTIVE_DPI) (shcore.dll, Windows 8.1+) or GetDpiForWindow (Win10 1607+); pick monitor by MonitorFromPoint/MonitorFromWindow(MONITOR_DEFAULTTONEAREST).
- Mixed-DPI overlay architecture: a single HWND has exactly ONE DPI, so a window spanning monitors with different scale factors renders its UI at one monitor's scale (and WPF additionally lays out in DIPs and can be bitmap-scaled on the other monitors); the robust pattern is one borderless topmost overlay window per monitor, each created at that monitor's physical bounds, all sharing the same frozen virtual-screen bitmap and one selection state in physical (virtual-screen) coordinates. WinForms/Win32 with PMv2 can get away with one spanning window for hit-testing because coordinates are physical, but per-monitor windows avoid the ShareX-class bugs and let crosshair/HUD text render at each monitor's DPI.
- Overlay self-exclusion: SetWindowDisplayAffinity(hwnd, WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE) (value 0x00000011) makes a window invisible to BitBlt/DDA/WGC capture — 'Starting in Windows 10 Version 2004, WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE is a supported value'; the window must belong to the calling process; on Windows 7-1909 the same call only supports WDA_MONITOR (window turns black in captures instead of disappearing). Use it if you capture live under the overlay; the freeze-frame pattern avoids needing it.
- Fullscreen capture of a specific monitor, three ways: (1) GDI — BitBlt the monitor's physical DesktopCoordinates from the screen DC (what ShareX does via Screen bounds / GetActiveScreenBounds for 'active monitor' capture); (2) WGC — IGraphicsCaptureItemInterop::CreateForMonitor(HMONITOR) (Win10 1903+); (3) DDA — find the IDXGIOutput whose DXGI_OUTPUT_DESC.Monitor equals the HMONITOR and call DuplicateOutput(1) on it. The newer WinRT statics GraphicsCaptureItem.TryCreateFromWindowId / TryCreateFromDisplayId (contract v12, build 20348+) do the same without COM interop.
- Windows 11 Snipping Tool: the Win+Shift+S / PrtScn snip overlay is hosted by ScreenClippingHost.exe located under C:\Windows\SystemApps\MicrosoftWindows.Client.CBS_cw5n1h2txyewy (earlier, Snip & Sketch's 'Screen snipping' experience shipped in the Microsoft.ScreenSketch store package since Win10 1809); the hotkey 'is tied into the OS shell and invokes the clipping overlay without a direct process launch that AppLocker can intercept' (MS Q&A). The Snipping Tool app itself (Microsoft.ScreenSketch package, SnippingTool.exe) is a packaged WinUI app that replaced the legacy Win32 GDI snippingtool.exe; its screen-recording feature (added 2023) shows the WGC-style capture border.
- Snipping Tool HDR: an 'HDR Screenshot Color Corrector' toggle was added to Snipping Tool settings in a 2024 update — it 'automatically adjusts the colors of screenshots captured on an HDR monitor' so they don't look washed out when saved as SDR PNGs (Settings > three-dot menu > 'HDR screenshot color corrector').
- Correct HDR-to-SDR handling for your own app: capture FP16 scRGB (WGC R16G16B16A16Float or DDA DuplicateOutput1), read the monitor's SDR white level (DISPLAYCONFIG_SDR_WHITE_LEVEL via QueryDisplayConfig; 1.0f in scRGB = 80 nits, so scale by SDRWhiteLevel/80), then tone-map — e.g. the Direct2D HDR tone map effect (D2D1 HdrToneMap, Windows 10 1809+) — before encoding to 8-bit PNG; community reference implementation: github.com/MagestiUA/HDR_Screenshot_tool_for_windows (DDA + tone mapping + clipboard).
- EnumWindows enumerates top-level windows in Z-order; for click-to-pick use WindowFromPoint + GetAncestor(GA_ROOT) (the overlay must be WS_EX_LAYERED|WS_EX_TRANSPARENT or hidden during hit-testing so it doesn't pick itself), or hit-test your pre-built filtered window list against DWMWA_EXTENDED_FRAME_BOUNDS rectangles top-down — the pre-built-list approach is what Greenshot/ShareX do because it also gives child/UWP-aware rectangles for the picker highlight.

## Recommendations
- Baseline: require Windows 10 1903+ (build 18362) so IGraphicsCaptureItemInterop::CreateForWindow/CreateForMonitor is always available; if you can require Windows 11 (22000+) you also get removable WGC borders (build 20348+ API). Windows 10 consumer support ended Oct 14, 2025, so a Win11-first baseline is defensible in 2026.
- Project setup (.NET 8/9): TargetFramework net8.0-windows10.0.19041.0 (or 10.0.22621.0) — the Windows SDK WinRT projections (CsWinRT) for Windows.Graphics.Capture come free with the TFM; add Microsoft.Windows.CsWin32 source generator for P/Invoke (BitBlt, PrintWindow, DwmGetWindowAttribute, EnumWindows, monitor/DPI APIs) and Vortice.Windows (SharpDX is unmaintained) or TerraFX.Interop.Windows for the D3D11/DXGI side of WGC frame handling; app.manifest with <dpiAwareness>PerMonitorV2</dpiAwareness>.
- Region + fullscreen stills: use the freeze-frame pattern — one BitBlt(SRCCOPY|CAPTUREBLT) of the entire virtual screen (physical pixels under PMv2), then show one borderless TopMost overlay window per monitor drawing its slice of the frozen bitmap; selection math stays in virtual-screen physical coordinates; crop the final rect from the frozen bitmap. This sidesteps every mixed-DPI overlay bug ShareX hit and needs no WGC session at all for the common path.
- Window capture: enumerate with EnumWindows filtered by IsWindowVisible + DWMWA_CLOAKED==0 + GetWindowTextLength>0 + !WS_EX_TOOLWINDOW (copy OBS's window-helpers.c rules); rectangles from DwmGetWindowAttribute(DWMWA_EXTENDED_FRAME_BOUNDS). Capture the picked window with WGC CreateForWindow (works occluded, no shadow, correct for UWP/Chromium); fall back to PrintWindow with PW_RENDERFULLCONTENT (0x2), then to cropping the frozen desktop bitmap. Offer 'include shadow' by compositing a synthetic shadow onto the shadowless capture (or Greenshot's white/black DwmRegisterThumbnail double-capture if you want the real alpha) rather than capturing background pixels.
- WGC border handling: on Windows 11 call GraphicsCaptureAccess.RequestAccessAsync(GraphicsCaptureAccessKind.Borderless) once (InitializeWithWindow for unpackaged apps), then set session.IsBorderRequired=false and session.IsCursorCaptureEnabled=false for stills; on Windows 10 either accept the momentary yellow border or route window capture through PrintWindow/frozen-bitmap crop instead.
- HDR: detect Advanced Color on the target monitor; if on, capture via WGC with DirectXPixelFormat.R16G16B16A16Float, scale by DISPLAYCONFIG_SDR_WHITE_LEVEL/80, tone-map (Direct2D HDR ToneMap effect) to sRGB before PNG-encoding — this is precisely the gap that makes plain BitBlt screenshots washed out and that Snipping Tool's 'HDR Screenshot Color Corrector' papers over.
- Skip DXGI Desktop Duplication entirely for a stills-first Skitch clone — it is monitor-only, needs rotation/pointer/adapter plumbing, and everything it offers for stills WGC does better; revisit it (or just reuse WGC) only if you add video/GIF recording later.
- If you ever draw a live (non-frozen) overlay while capturing, mark the overlay windows with SetWindowDisplayAffinity(WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE) (Win10 2004+) so they never appear in your own captures.

- Whether classic ShareX 17.x (WinForms, pre-Avalonia) declares any DPI awareness in its manifest/app.config — the Avalonia master manifest has none; the classic branch wasn't inspected.

# TOPIC: shell-integration

## Summary
Researched Windows shell-integration techniques for a WPF tray screenshot app across eight areas: system tray (H.NotifyIcon.Wpf is the maintained fork of Hardcodet, with TaskbarCreated/GUID gotchas), global hotkeys (RegisterHotKey works for normal combos but Win+Shift+S fails with ERROR_HOTKEY_ALREADY_REGISTERED and PrintScreen is OS-claimed unless the Win11 'PrintScreenKeyForSnippingEnabled' setting is off or a WH_KEYBOARD_LL hook is used), Share sheet (IDataTransferManagerInterop GUID 3A3DCD6C-3EAB-43DC-BCDE-45671CE800C8 with GetForWindow/ShowShareUIForWindow, DataRequested + SetBitmap(RandomAccessStreamReference) + deferral, 200 ms constraint), clipboard (write PNG named format + CF_DIB + CF_BITMAP together; CF_DIB has no alpha so transparency renders black/opaque, CF_DIBV5 support is inconsistent, WPF Clipboard.SetImage alone breaks paste in Paint/SnagIt and CLIPBRD_E_CANT_OPEN needs a retry loop), drag-out (CF_HDROP with a real temp file is the universally compatible path since WPF DataObject can't do virtual files; virtual-file CFSTR_FILEDESCRIPTORW/CFSTR_FILECONTENTS via a custom COM IDataObject like VirtualFileDataObject works in Explorer/Outlook and Chromium supports it via TYMED_ISTREAM/ISTORAGE), run-at-login (HKCU\...\CurrentVersion\Run string value, disabled state mirrored in StartupApproved\Run), single-instance (Global\ Mutex + named pipe, AllowSetForegroundWindow handoff to beat SetForegroundWindow restrictions), and toasts (Microsoft.Toolkit.Uwp.Notifications 7.0+ on net6.0-windows10.0.17763.0+, ToastNotificationManagerCompat auto-registers unpackaged apps, local-file images only).

## Facts
- TRAY: WPF has no built-in NotifyIcon; the classic library is hardcodet/wpf-notifyicon (NuGet Hardcodet.NotifyIcon.Wpf / community Hardcodet.NotifyIcon.Wpf.NetCore 1.1.5), described as 'a purely independent control' not relying on the WinForms NotifyIcon, supporting rich tooltips, popups, context menus, and balloon messages (github.com/hardcodet/wpf-notifyicon).
- TRAY: H.NotifyIcon (github.com/HavenDV/H.NotifyIcon) is the actively maintained continuation of the inactive Hardcodet base project for .NET 6+; WPF package is 'H.NotifyIcon.Wpf' (also H.NotifyIcon.WinUI, H.NotifyIcon core for console apps). Features: GeneratedIconSource (dynamic icons from text/emoji/colors/gradients without image files), Windows 11 efficiency-mode API (EfficiencyModeUtilities.SetEfficiencyMode(), WindowExtensions.Hide/Show(), TaskbarIcon.ForceCreate()), ContextMenuThemeMode (System/Light/Dark/Default).
- TRAY GOTCHA (icon GUID): H.NotifyIcon's default tray-icon GUID 'is a hash function that creates a unique Guid based on the path to your file, because Windows associates the guid with the current path' — if the exe path changes, Windows loses the saved tray-visibility preference unless the binary is Authenticode-signed. Without any GUID (NIF_GUID), the icon identity is tied to the executable path.
- TRAY GOTCHA (Explorer restart): when Explorer restarts the taskbar is recreated and tray icons vanish; you must RegisterWindowMessage("TaskbarCreated") and re-add the icon (Shell_NotifyIcon NIM_ADD) when that message arrives. Counter-gotcha: after RDP reconnect Windows may auto-restore icons, so the re-add can fail because the icon already exists — handle the 'already exists' failure (e.g., NIM_MODIFY fallback). (learn.microsoft.com Q&A 1316178; openhardwaremonitor issue #1092).
- HOTKEYS: RegisterHotKey(hWnd, id, fsModifiers, vk) — if hWnd is NULL, WM_HOTKEY is posted to the calling thread's message queue and must be pumped; the call 'fails if you try to associate a hot key with a window created by another thread'. id must be 0x0000–0xBFFF for apps (0xC000–0xFFFF via GlobalAddAtom for shared DLLs). MOD_NOREPEAT = 0x4000 suppresses auto-repeat notifications (not supported on Vista). MOD_ALT=0x0001, MOD_CONTROL=0x0002, MOD_SHIFT=0x0004, MOD_WIN=0x0008. (learn.microsoft.com RegisterHotKey doc, updated 2024-06-11).
- HOTKEYS: per the RegisterHotKey docs, 'Keyboard shortcuts that involve the WINDOWS key are reserved for use by the operating system', and 'The F12 key is reserved for use by the debugger at all times, so it should not be registered as a hot key.'
- HOTKEYS (PrintScreen): the docs state 'some pre-existing, default hotkeys registered by the OS (such as PrintScreen, which launches the Snipping tool) may be overridden by another hot key registration when one of the app's windows is in the foreground' — i.e., RegisterHotKey(VK_SNAPSHOT=0x2C) can take PrintScreen, and it wins over Snipping Tool. Snipping Tool only claims PrintScreen when the user setting 'Use the Print screen key to open screen capture' is on; it is stored per-user at HKCU\Control Panel\Keyboard, REG_DWORD PrintScreenKeyForSnippingEnabled (1 = Snipping Tool intercepts, 0 = free for third parties). A logoff/logon or restart is typically needed for hotkey registration changes to fully reset.
- HOTKEYS (Win11 policy): a Group Policy named 'Make Print Screen key yieldable' (Computer Configuration > Administrative Templates > Windows Components > File Explorer) appeared in Windows 11 Dev Channel preview builds (build 26300 referenced, early-2026 previews); Enabled means 'third-party screenshot tools and utilities are allowed to register handlers that take precedence over the OS's configured screenshot behavior' (windowsforum.com thread 403689).
- HOTKEYS (Win+Shift+S): cannot be bound with RegisterHotKey — 'Windows Explorer monopolizes this combination at system startup, causing standard RegisterHotKey calls to fail (ERROR_HOTKEY_ALREADY_REGISTERED)' (flameshot issue #4534). Workarounds: (a) a WH_KEYBOARD_LL low-level keyboard hook that intercepts VK_SNAPSHOT and Win+Shift+S and 'virtually releases modifier keys' to avoid stuck-modifier issues (a ~17 KB C++ daemon doing this is cited in the flameshot issue); (b) user-side registry: HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Explorer\Advanced, String value DisabledHotkeys = 'S' disables Win+S and Win+Shift+S (comma/letter list like 'S,C' disables more; requires restart) — note it kills plain Win+S search too.
- SHARE SHEET: DataTransferManager.GetForCurrentView/ShowShareUI don't work from Win32/WPF; you must use the COM interop interface IDataTransferManagerInterop, [ComImport] Guid("3A3DCD6C-3EAB-43DC-BCDE-45671CE800C8"), InterfaceType(ComInterfaceType.InterfaceIsIUnknown), with methods GetForWindow([In] IntPtr appWindow, [In] ref Guid riid) returning a DataTransferManager, and ShowShareUIForWindow(IntPtr appWindow). The riid to pass is the DataTransferManager interface GUID: a5caee9b-8708-49d1-8d36-67d25a8da00c. Microsoft's reference implementation: Windows-classic-samples/Samples/ShareSource/wpf/DataTransferManagerHelper.cs.
- SHARE SHEET: flow is GetForWindow(hwnd) -> subscribe DataRequested -> ShowShareUIForWindow(hwnd). In the DataRequested handler for async data (e.g., an image), call args.Request.GetDeferral(), set DataPackage properties, request.Data.SetBitmap(RandomAccessStreamReference.CreateFromStream/CreateFromUri(...)) (also usable for Properties.Thumbnail), then deferral.Complete(). Constraint: the DataPackage must be returned within 200 ms or the share operation times out (learn.microsoft.com DataRequest.GetDeferral).
- SHARE SHEET GOTCHA (CsWinRT): microsoft/CsWinRT issue #970 — in WinUI3/CsWinRT desktop apps, DataTransferManager.As<IDataTransferManagerInterop>() with a CsWin32-generated interop interface returned an object that 'cannot be cast to DataTransferManager' (cast always null); the fix is to declare the ComImport interface yourself with GetForWindow returning the projected DataTransferManager type (marshalled via WinRT), as in Microsoft's DataTransferManagerHelper.cs sample, rather than relying on CsWin32's generated definition.
- CLIPBOARD: multiple bitmap formats coexist; Windows auto-synthesizes CF_DIB and CF_BITMAP from CF_DIBV5 (and vice versa). Best practice: 'place CF_BITMAP, CF_DIB and CF_DIBV5 versions of images on the clipboard; if you don't, they will be synthesized by the clipboard' and synthesis of alpha images is often wrong — supply your own versions since you know the image has alpha.
- CLIPBOARD TRANSPARENCY PITFALL: CF_DIB (BI_RGB) has no alpha — 'the high order byte (alpha) is not used; if it's 0x00 (transparent), it will be rendered as opaque with a black background'. CF_DIBV5 (BITMAPV5HEADER, packed DIB from a DIBSECTION) preserves alpha by design, but many receivers implement DIBV5 alpha poorly ('vague documentation'); Word 'will not correctly identify an alpha channel bitmap' pasted from the clipboard. Practical rule: flatten the CF_DIB copy onto white (or the likely background) yourself instead of letting alpha bytes leak through.
- CLIPBOARD PNG FORMAT: register/set a format literally named "PNG" containing the raw PNG stream — e.g., var data = new DataObject(); data.SetData("PNG", pngMemoryStream); Clipboard.SetDataObject(data, true); — this preserves transparency for receivers that support it (Word, Chromium browsers, many Electron apps); support varies per app, so ship PNG alongside CF_DIB/CF_BITMAP for Paint/legacy apps.
- CLIPBOARD (WPF specifics, Rick Strahl 'Wrestling with Clipboard Images in WPF'): WPF Clipboard.SetImage() alone produces images that paste blank/invalid in SnagIt and classic Paint ('correct pixel dimensions' but unrenderable); Chromium browsers and Firefox handle WPF-set images. Reading side: check formats in order PNG stream first, then WPF Clipboard.GetImage() (preserves transparency), detect Firefox via 'text/_moz_htmlinfo', then System.Drawing.Bitmap, last resort System.Windows.Forms.Clipboard.GetImage() which 'does not support transparency'.
- CLIPBOARD RELIABILITY: OpenClipboard/SetData intermittently fails with 0x800401D0 CLIPBRD_E_CANT_OPEN (WPF Clipboard.SetText 'takes several hundred milliseconds and then fails ... in the flush operation' — dotnet/wpf issue #9901); cause is another process (clipboard managers, Win+V clipboard history, Ditto/ClipX) briefly holding the clipboard. Standard mitigation: retry loop with short delays around SetDataObject; also Clipboard.SetDataObject(data, copy: true) flushes so data survives app exit — flush is the slow/contended part.
- DRAG-OUT (simple/compatible): WPF's built-in DataObject supports only real files for file drags — DataObject.SetFileDropList / DataFormats.FileDrop (CF_HDROP) 'only accepts paths to existing files'; so the pragmatic approach for a screenshot app is: save the rendered PNG to a temp file, put its path in a FileDropList, call DragDrop.DoDragDrop(source, dataObject, DragDropEffects.Copy). CF_HDROP is accepted by Explorer, browsers (as file upload), Slack, etc. DoDragDrop is synchronous and blocks the UI thread until the drop completes.
- DRAG-OUT (virtual files): true virtual-file drag uses CFSTR_FILEDESCRIPTOR ('the structure is called FILEGROUPDESCRIPTOR, but the clipboard format is CFSTR_FILEDESCRIPTOR without the group') + CFSTR_FILECONTENTS, which requires implementing COM IDataObject yourself (System.Runtime.InteropServices.ComTypes.IDataObject, e.g. derive from WinForms DataObject and override GetData for delayed rendering). The canonical .NET implementation is Delay's VirtualFileDataObject (dlaa.me/blog/post/9913083): SetData(short dataFormat, int index, Action<Stream> streamData) streams content on demand via TYMED_ISTREAM so 'contents of this file aren't generated until they're actually required by the drop target'; HGLOBAL mode exists for upfront data.
- DRAG-OUT (browser targets): Chromium implements virtual-file drops — it reads CFSTR_FILEDESCRIPTORW/CFSTR_FILECONTENTS (GetVirtualFilenames in OSExchangeDataProviderWin/ClipboardUtil), and on drop WebContentsViewAura::OnPerformDrop streams contents to temp files; 'for a drop target to read file contents, they must be streamed from the drag source using either IStorage (TYMED_ISTORAGE) or IStream (TYMED_ISTREAM)' (chromium.googlesource.com commit e524176b; originally for Outlook message/attachment drags, crbug 322605). So virtual files work into Chrome/Edge/Electron, but CF_HDROP with a real temp file remains the most universal.
- RUN AT LOGIN: add a String value (name = app name, data = quoted exe path) under HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run (per-user, no admin) or HKLM\...\Run (all users, needs admin). If the program requires admin rights, a Run-key entry 'will either prompt for UAC or fail silently' — Task Scheduler with an 'At log on' trigger and 'Run with highest privileges' is the route for elevated autostart; the Startup folder is the user-visible alternative for simple apps.
- RUN AT LOGIN (disable-state gotcha): Task Manager's Startup tab and Settings > Apps > Startup toggles do NOT delete your Run value — they record enabled/disabled state as binary values under HKCU (or HKLM)\SOFTWARE\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Explorer\StartupApproved\Run; 'the values below the StartupApproved\Run registry key can be used to enable or disable the corresponding values under the standard Run registry key', so check StartupApproved before assuming your Run entry is active.
- SINGLE INSTANCE: standard .NET pattern = named Mutex (prefix name with Global\ + a GUID to enforce across sessions; keep the Mutex object alive for the app's lifetime, e.g., a static field, or GC will release it) checked in WPF App.OnStartup, plus a NamedPipeServerStream in the first instance; second instances connect as pipe clients, send their args (e.g., 'show capture UI'), and exit; first instance marshals handling onto the Dispatcher.
- SINGLE INSTANCE (foreground activation): SetForegroundWindow 'is restricted by the OS to callers other than the foreground process' — calling it from the already-running instance yields only a flashing taskbar button. Correct handoff: the NEW instance (which has foreground rights because the user launched it) calls AllowSetForegroundWindow(pid-of-first-instance) (or ASFW_ANY) before signaling over the pipe; then the first instance can Activate/SetForegroundWindow successfully (Rick Strahl 'Window Activation Headaches in WPF', 2020).
- TOASTS: use NuGet Microsoft.Toolkit.Uwp.Notifications v7.0+ ('supports all C# app types, including WPF, UWP, WinForms, and Console, even without packaging your app as MSIX'); on .NET 6+ the TFM must be net6.0-windows10.0.17763.0 or greater. Send with new ToastContentBuilder().AddText(...).AddInlineImage(...).Show(); ToastNotificationManagerCompat handles AUMID/COM-activator registration automatically for unpackaged apps.
- TOASTS (activation): subscribe ToastNotificationManagerCompat.OnActivated in WPF App startup (App.xaml.cs OnStartup); if the app is running the event fires on a background thread (dispatch to UI thread for UI work); if the app is closed, clicking the toast launches the EXE with ToastNotificationManagerCompat.WasCurrentProcessToastActivated() == true, then OnActivated fires. Known issue microsoft-ui-xaml #6133: OnActivated not invoked in some closed-app toast-action scenarios.
- TOASTS (images + cleanup): 'Unpackaged apps don't support HTTP images; you must download the image to your local app data and reference it locally' (file path / file:/// URIs only) — relevant for showing the captured screenshot thumbnail in the post-capture toast. Call ToastNotificationManagerCompat.Uninstall() from your uninstaller to remove the registration and pending notifications. If registering manually without the compat library (raw CreateToastNotifier("MyAumid")), the AUMID must be at most 129 characters and registered in the registry with display name, icon, and a COM activator GUID.
- TOASTS (alternative API): Windows App SDK's AppNotificationManager is the newer converged API (WindowsAppSDK issue #137 tracked the convergence), but Microsoft.Toolkit.Uwp.Notifications remains the documented path for plain unpackaged WPF without taking a Windows App SDK dependency.

## Recommendations
- Tray: use H.NotifyIcon.Wpf; set an explicit, stable tray GUID only if the install path is fixed or the exe is signed; handle TaskbarCreated re-add with an 'already exists' fallback.
- Hotkeys: use RegisterHotKey with MOD_NOREPEAT for the app's own combos (e.g., Ctrl+Shift+S variants and PrintScreen via VK_SNAPSHOT); do not attempt Win+Shift+S via RegisterHotKey — if capturing it is a hard requirement, add an optional WH_KEYBOARD_LL hook mode that swallows the combo and synthesizes modifier key-ups.
- Clipboard: write four formats together in one DataObject — 'PNG' stream, CF_DIB flattened onto white, CF_BITMAP, and optionally CF_DIBV5 — and wrap SetDataObject(data, true) in a 3-5 attempt retry loop for CLIPBRD_E_CANT_OPEN.
- Drag-out: implement CF_HDROP with a temp PNG file first (works everywhere including Slack and browsers); add VirtualFileDataObject-style CFSTR_FILEDESCRIPTORW/CFSTR_FILECONTENTS later only if avoiding temp files matters; don't delete the temp file until well after DoDragDrop returns.
- Share sheet: copy Microsoft's DataTransferManagerHelper.cs (declare IDataTransferManagerInterop yourself; don't rely on CsWin32's projection), use GetDeferral + SetBitmap(RandomAccessStreamReference) and keep data preparation under 200 ms by pre-encoding the PNG before showing the UI.
- Run-at-login: HKCU Run key value plus a read of StartupApproved\Run to reflect true enabled state in the settings UI.
- Single instance: Global\{GUID} Mutex + named pipe; have the second instance call AllowSetForegroundWindow before signaling so the first instance can bring its capture UI to front.
- Toasts: Microsoft.Toolkit.Uwp.Notifications 7.x on net8.0-windows10.0.17763.0+, save the capture thumbnail to %LOCALAPPDATA% for the toast image, subscribe OnActivated at startup, and call ToastNotificationManagerCompat.Uninstall() in the uninstaller.

## Open questions
- Whether the Windows 11 'Make Print Screen key yieldable' group policy has shipped to stable Windows 11 (only Dev Channel build 26300 / early-2026 previews were confirmed) and its exact ADMX/registry backing value.
- Exact per-app PNG-vs-DIBV5 clipboard consumption behavior of Slack and Discord specifically (Electron/Chromium generally reads the PNG format, but no authoritative per-app documentation was found).
- Whether ShowShareUIForWindow reliably shows the share sheet for fully unpackaged (no identity) apps on current Windows 11 builds — the interop pattern is documented and sampled by Microsoft, but package-identity-related edge cases weren't conclusively documented.
- The MS Learn 'send-local-toast' page moved (both known URLs returned 404), so toast facts were assembled from search excerpts of the docs plus the NuGet/GitHub sources rather than one canonical page.

# TOPIC: stack-choice

## Summary
Ranked recommendation for a solo-dev Skitch-clone Windows tray app: (1) .NET 8/9 WPF self-contained single-file — the only stack where all four hard requirements (transparent click-through overlay, per-monitor-DPI v2, vector canvas with in-place editable text, deep Windows interop incl. Share sheet/clipboard/drag-drop) are first-class and battle-proven by the entire Windows screenshot-tool ecosystem (ShareX, Greenshot are .NET); its only real costs are a ~70MB compressed / ~150MB extracted single exe and a 2-3s cold start that is paid once at login because a tray app stays resident, after which the capture overlay is warm-process instant. (2) Avalonia 11 — same C# velocity plus NativeAOT (faster startup, smaller exe), per-monitor DPI by default and built-in TrayIcon, but click-through overlays need Win32 interop and Share-sheet needs CsWinRT plumbing. (3) Qt Widgets — QGraphicsScene with in-place-editable QGraphicsTextItem is the ideal annotation canvas and powers Flameshot/Ksnip, ~20MB static exe possible, but LGPL static-link compliance or commercial licensing plus C++ maintenance burden hurt a solo dev, and even Flameshot has open mixed-DPI capture bugs. (4) Tauri 2 — smallest exe (2-10MB), fastest start (~0.4s), and the web's unmatched annotation-canvas libraries (fabric.js/tldraw/Excalidraw-style), but click-through is all-or-nothing setIgnoreCursorEvents requiring a ~60fps cursor-polling hack, WebView2 CSS-pixel vs physical-pixel math across mixed-DPI monitors is error-prone, and drag-out/Share need plugins. (5) Rust native (Slint/egui/iced) — tiny instant binaries but in-place text editing on a canvas, IME, and accessibility are the ecosystem's weakest points. (6) WinUI 3 — disqualified for this app class: no AllowsTransparency equivalent (open issue #7276), +200MB self-contained, single-exe only when unpackaged+self-contained with temp-dir extraction. (7) Electron — 80-200MB installers, 200-300MB idle RAM, portable single exe re-extracts to temp on each run; wrong tool for a lightweight tray utility.

## Facts
- WPF trimming is disabled in the .NET SDK: Microsoft's 'Known trimming incompatibilities' doc states WPF 'makes substantial use of reflection', 'almost no WPF apps are runnable after trimming, so trimming support for WPF is currently disabled', and WinForms trimming is likewise disabled due to reliance on built-in COM marshalling — so NativeAOT is off the table for both (learn.microsoft.com/dotnet/core/deploying/trimming/incompatibilities).
- WPF self-contained single-file exe realistic size: dotnet/wpf issue #3070 reports extracted single-exe sizes reaching ~150MB; framework-dependent publish is only a few MB but requires the .NET Desktop Runtime preinstalled.
- WPF self-contained cold start: dotnet/runtime issue #78379 ('Self contained WPF app's cold start can be 2-3 seconds') documents 2-3s first launch after Windows boot even on high-end machines; warm starts drop to under 1s (one test showed ~8-10s cold vs <1s warm). ReadyToRun and MulticoreJIT are the sanctioned mitigations.
- Key reframe for the 'tray app must feel instant' requirement: a tray app pays cold start once at login and stays resident; the latency that matters is showing the capture overlay from a warm process, which every stack (including WPF) does in well under a second.
- WPF transparent click-through overlay is a solved problem: Window with AllowsTransparency=true + transparent background, plus WS_EX_TRANSPARENT/WS_EX_LAYERED extended styles for input passthrough — the standard technique used by screen-capture/recorder apps.
- WPF per-monitor DPI: supported via app.manifest declaring PerMonitorV2 ('Developing a Per-Monitor DPI-Aware WPF Application', learn.microsoft.com).
- WPF Windows Share sheet interop: desktop (Win32/WinForms/WPF) apps must use the COM interface IDataTransferManagerInterop with GetForWindow(hwnd) and ShowShareUIForWindow instead of DataTransferManager.ShowShareUI; Microsoft ships a WPF ShareSource sample (Windows-classic-samples/Samples/ShareSource/wpf/DataTransferManagerHelper.cs).
- Precedent: the dominant Windows screenshot/annotation tools are .NET — ShareX and Greenshot are C#/WinForms; Flameshot, Ksnip, and Snipaste-style tools are Qt.
- Avalonia NativeAOT is officially supported: set PublishAot=true on the executable project, IsAotCompatible=true on all libraries, BuiltInComInteropSupport=false pre-12.0; docs cite faster startup, reduced memory, self-contained deployment, and 'smaller distribution size when combined with trimming' (docs.avaloniaui.net/docs/deployment/native-aot).
- Avalonia size data points: a blank Avalonia 11.0.9 app + Avalonia.ReactiveUI publishes to ~80MB single-file, while a realistic app with many third-party dependencies came to ~26MB with a normal platform-specific release publish (AvaloniaUI/Avalonia discussion #14633).
- Avalonia startup history: issue #5242 measured a hello-world at 2.4s to first window, improved to 1.4s with PublishReadyToRun=true, vs <0.5s for WinForms — startup is a known Avalonia weak spot that NativeAOT mitigates.
- Avalonia is per-monitor DPI-aware by default on Windows; all layout uses device-independent pixels, Screen.Scaling reports each display's factor, and the docs warn that an app.manifest overriding DPI awareness breaks it (docs.avaloniaui.net platform-specific guides/troubleshooting).
- Avalonia has built-in TrayIcon support (works without a main window, per discussion #10960) but click-through transparent windows are not first-class: issue #4956 ('Make content nonclickable in Transparent Window') shows achieving input-transparent regions requires Win32 workarounds.
- WinUI 3 cannot create transparent windows: there is no AllowsTransparency equivalent (open feature request microsoft/microsoft-ui-xaml #7276 and #10311); workarounds require DwmExtendFrameIntoClientArea/DwmEnableBlurBehindWindow plus WndProc subclassing — a fundamental problem for a region-selection overlay app.
- WinUI 3 single exe: PublishSingleFile is supported only for unpackaged AND self-contained apps (Windows App SDK 1.5+); dependencies extract to a temp directory at first launch; framework-dependent and packaged apps do not support it; self-contained Windows App SDK adds ~200MB to an empty project; unpackaged apps have no package identity so they are not Store-eligible as MSIX.
- Windows App SDK 1.6 (Sept 2024) added Native AOT support for WinUI 3: Microsoft measured 50% start-time reduction, ~8x package-size reduction with framework package, ~2x reduction in self-contained mode (Contoso Camera sample, Windows Developer Blog).
- Qt licensing for a closed-source solo app: Qt is dual-licensed LGPL/commercial; static linking under LGPL makes the app arguably no longer a 'work that uses the library', requiring you to provide object files so users can relink — a real compliance burden for a proprietary exe; dynamic linking avoids this but means shipping Qt DLLs; a statically linked optimized Qt Widgets exe can come in under ~20MB.
- Qt Widgets' QGraphicsScene is the canonical vector-annotation canvas (Flameshot uses Qt; Ksnip is 'a Qt based cross-platform screenshot tool' with many annotation features), yet even Flameshot has an open bug for mixed-DPI multi-monitor setups on Windows (flameshot-org/flameshot issue #4495) — evidence that mixed-DPI capture overlays are hard even in mature Qt tools.
- Tauri 2 sizes/startup from 2025-2026 comparisons: Tauri apps ship at 2-10MB vs Electron 80-200MB; a to-do benchmark launched in 0.4s using 28MB RAM (Tauri) vs 1.5s and 250MB RAM (Electron); Tauri idles ~30-40MB vs Electron 200-300MB.
- Tauri uses Edge WebView2 on Windows: preinstalled on Windows 11 but possibly absent on Windows 10, requiring a bootstrapper in the installer.
- Tauri overlay limitation: setIgnoreCursorEvents(true) passes ALL cursor events through the window — Tauri exposes no per-region hit-testing, so overlay apps use a Rust loop polling cursor position at ~60fps and toggling setIgnoreCursorEvents depending on whether the cursor is over UI (tauri issues #2090, #6164; Manasight overlay blog).
- Tauri has no built-in interactive screenshot/region-capture API (open feature request tauri-apps/tauri #12501); capture must be done in Rust (e.g., screenshots/windows-capture crates).
- Tauri drag-out requires plugins: tauri-plugin-drag / tauri-plugin-drag-as-window (crates.io) enable starting OS drags of files on Windows/macOS/Linux; not built into core.
- Electron portable single-exe (electron-builder 'portable' target via NSIS) extracts to a random %TEMP% path on each run (electron-builder issues #1612, #3612), adding startup latency; normal distribution is an installer above 100MB.
- Rust GUI text-editing reality check: a 2025 survey of 43 Rust GUI crates (boringcactus.com) building a label+input app found most libraries including Floem and iced lack Narrator (screen reader) support and have incomplete accessibility; egui produces the smallest binaries; iced's multi-line text_editor widget is comparatively new; a Jan 2026 Medium benchmark of egui/iced/Slint/GTK concluded the author returned to Electron.
- Slint licensing: triple-licensed GPLv3 / Royalty-Free / Commercial; the Royalty-Free Desktop-Mobile-Web license v2.0 permits proprietary desktop apps at no cost provided you disclose Slint usage (e.g., AboutSlint widget or badge); embedded is excluded from the free tier (slint.dev/pricing, LICENSE.md).
- Flutter Windows: engine overhead ~10-15MB minimum; builds a Release-directory exe (not single-file by default — needs zip/installer or MSIX via msix package); Skia rendering; transparency/acrylic requires third-party plugins (window_manager setOpacity/backgroundColor, flutter_acrylic which calls the undocumented SetWindowCompositionAttribute user32 API); multi-window needs desktop_multi_window with a separate Flutter engine per window; historical multi-monitor DPI gaps (flutter issues #30728, #77605).
- Code signing / SmartScreen (critical for distributing any unsigned exe): EV certificates' instant SmartScreen bypass was removed in 2024 — EV now builds reputation the same as OV, so the $400+/yr EV premium is no longer justified for SmartScreen alone; unsigned files restart reputation from zero on every new version; reputation transfers across versions only with a consistent publisher identity; Azure Trusted Signing (renamed Azure Artifact Signing) costs $9.99/mo Basic (5,000 signatures) or $99.99/mo Premium, available to individual developers only in USA/Canada, and still requires weeks of clean installs to build SmartScreen reputation (learn.microsoft.com code-signing-options and smartscreen-reputation; melatonin.dev).
- MSIX packaging trade-off: MSIX gives clean install/uninstall and Store distribution (Store signs for you, bypassing SmartScreen reputation grind) but WinUI-3-unpackaged single-exe and MSIX are mutually exclusive; plain exe distribution demands your own signing + reputation building.
- Canvas-with-editable-text capability ranking: WPF (TextBox/RichTextBox as real elements on Canvas with adorners — first-class), Qt (QGraphicsTextItem editable in place — first-class), Avalonia (same element-on-Canvas model as WPF — first-class), Tauri/Electron (web canvas libraries like fabric.js/tldraw/Excalidraw are the most polished annotation canvases anywhere), Flutter (CustomPainter + overlaid TextField — workable), Rust egui/iced/Slint (weakest: immediate-mode or DSL text editing with IME/accessibility gaps).

## Recommendations
- RANK 1 — .NET 8/9 WPF, self-contained single-file with ReadyToRun and compression: it is the only stack where the four make-or-break features (click-through transparent overlay via AllowsTransparency + WS_EX_TRANSPARENT, PerMonitorV2 DPI via manifest, editable TextBox-on-Canvas vector annotation, and Share-sheet/clipboard/drag-drop via IDataTransferManagerInterop and built-in APIs) are all first-class, documented, and proven by ShareX/Greenshot-class tools; accept the ~70MB compressed/150MB extracted exe and the once-per-login 2-3s cold start (start minimized at login so the overlay is always warm), and highest solo-dev velocity in C#/XAML with the largest body of screenshot-tool sample code.
- RANK 2 — Avalonia 11 with NativeAOT if exe size and cold start matter more than interop convenience: keeps C#/XAML productivity, gets genuinely fast AOT startup and a smaller binary, per-monitor DPI by default, built-in TrayIcon; budget extra days for Win32 interop (WS_EX_TRANSPARENT click-through, IDataTransferManagerInterop via CsWinRT) that WPF gives for free.
- RANK 3 — Qt Widgets (C++ or PySide6) only if already fluent in Qt: QGraphicsScene is the best-designed annotation canvas of any stack and Flameshot/Ksnip prove the app category, but LGPL static-linking compliance (or commercial license cost) plus C++ build/deploy overhead make it a worse solo-dev deal than the .NET options, and Flameshot's open mixed-DPI bug shows Qt doesn't get DPI right for free either.
- RANK 4 — Tauri 2 if you want to leverage web annotation canvases (fabric.js/tldraw/Excalidraw-fork) and a 2-10MB download: prototype the two risk areas FIRST — (a) full-screen overlay with per-region click-through via the setIgnoreCursorEvents 60fps-polling hack, (b) mixed-DPI region-to-physical-pixel mapping — and only proceed if both pass on a 3-monitor mixed-scaling rig; add tauri-plugin-drag for drag-out and Rust-side windows-capture for grabbing frames.
- AVOID for this app: WinUI 3 (no transparent windows — issue #7276 — plus ~200MB self-contained and temp-extraction single-exe; disqualifying for an overlay-centric tool), Electron (80-200MB, 200-300MB idle RAM, temp-extracting portable exe — wrong shape for a tray utility), Flutter desktop (overlay/acrylic depends on undocumented-API plugins, per-engine multi-window, DPI gaps), and Rust-native GUIs for v1 (in-place text editing, IME, and accessibility on canvas are the ecosystem's weakest points).
- Regardless of stack: sign with Azure Artifact/Trusted Signing ($9.99/mo Basic tier, available to US individual developers) from the very first public build with a stable publisher identity so SmartScreen reputation accrues across versions; do NOT pay for EV (its instant-reputation bypass was removed in 2024); expect several weeks of warnings for early downloaders or ship via Microsoft Store/MSIX to skip the reputation grind.
- Architecture note for the overlay in any stack: capture the screen first (DXGI/GDI or windows-capture), then show the frozen frame in the full-screen selection window — this sidesteps most live click-through complexity and mixed-DPI cursor math, and is what mature tools do.

# TOPIC: prior-art

## Summary
Surveyed the four major open-source screenshot annotators. ShareX (GPL-3.0, C# WinForms migrating to Avalonia) uses a self-invalidating fullscreen borderless form with a ShapeManager holding List<BaseShape>, 9 ResizeNode handles, memento-based ImageEditorHistory, and effect shapes (blur/pixelate) that stay live-previewed via cached bitmaps and are only baked in at RenderOutputImage(). Greenshot (GPL-3.0, C# WinForms) has the cleanest object model for a WPF clone to study: Surface : Control holds IDrawableContainerList, undo is dual Stack<IMemento> with merge support, DrawableContainer carries 8 ResizeAdorner handles plus an attachable IFilter chain — ObfuscateContainer swaps BlurFilter/PixelizationFilter, which re-blur the live background region on every paint (fully non-destructive until GetImageForExport() flattens). Flameshot (GPLv3+, C++/Qt) keeps annotations as a re-playable CaptureTool object stack over the pristine screenshot, undo via QUndoStack of whole-stack snapshots, and offers a "secure pixelate" that synthesizes output from fringe pixels so content is unrecoverable. All three are copyleft: a proprietary clone may learn architecture/algorithms (ideas aren't copyrightable) but must not copy, translate, or closely paraphrase code — clean-room discipline recommended. Permissive resources that CAN be copied: swappy (MIT, C/GTK/cairo blur+shapes), Text Grab (MIT, WPF fullscreen overlay), PowerToys (MIT), Core2D/Draw2D (MIT C# vector editor with shapes/handles/undo), ScreenToGif (MS-PL WPF editor), drweb86/annotator (licensee's choice of any OSI license; Avalonia+SkiaSharp). kImageAnnotator (LGPL-3.0, Qt) is the only purpose-built reusable annotation-canvas library found; no mature MIT/Apache annotation canvas exists for .NET/WPF — closest are Core2D and WPF's built-in InkCanvas/Adorner framework.

## Facts
- ShareX license: GPL-3.0 (LICENSE.txt in repo is the verbatim GNU GPL Version 3, dated June 29, 2007); main solution projects: ShareX.ScreenCaptureLib, ShareX.ImageEffectsLib, ShareX.HelpersLib, ShareX.HistoryLib, ShareX.ImageEditor, ShareX.UploadersLib, ShareX.MediaLib, ShareX.IndexerLib, plus a new ShareX.Avalonia project — the repo is migrating from WinForms to Avalonia for cross-platform.
- ShareX region-select overlay (RegionCaptureForm.cs, ShareX.ScreenCaptureLib/Forms): FormBorderStyle.None, Bounds = ScreenBounds (entire virtual screen), TopMost = true, ShowInTaskbar = false; screenshot taken via new Screenshot().CaptureRectangle(ScreenBounds) stored as 'Canvas' and drawn with a TextureBrush using g.CompositingMode = CompositingMode.SourceCopy.
- ShareX render loop: no timer — OnPaint() calls Update() then ends with 'if (!pause) { Invalidate(); }' for continuous self-invalidated redraw, throttled by an FPSManager honoring Options.FPSLimit; double buffering via SetStyle(ControlStyles.OptimizedDoubleBuffer | ControlStyles.UserPaint | ControlStyles.AllPaintingInWmPaint, true).
- ShareX dimming outside selection: when Options.BackgroundDimStrength > 0 a DimmedCanvas (semi-transparent black overlay) is drawn; the undimmed original shows through by setting g.Clip = new Region(regionDrawPath) and filling with backgroundHighlightBrush (a brush over the unmodified canvas).
- ShareX annotation object model (ShapeManager.cs): public List<BaseShape> Shapes; CurrentShape and CurrentHoverShape properties; filtered LINQ views DrawingShapes, EffectShapes, ToolShapes, Regions/ValidRegions; hit-testing via GetIntersectShape() iterating the shape list backwards (topmost first) calling shape.Intersects(position).
- ShareX handles: nine ResizeNode objects in ResizeNodes[] (ImageEditorControl instances in DrawableObjects); selection calls shape.ShowNodes(); during drag ShapeManager.UpdateNodes() calls shape.OnNodeUpdate() while mouse is down and shape.OnResized() when done.
- ShareX BaseShape: RectangleF Rectangle kept in sync with StartPosition/EndPosition; abstract ShapeCategory and ShapeType; IsValidShape requires Rectangle.Width/Height >= Options.MinimumSize; default hit-test is Rectangle.Contains(position); Move(float x, float y) and Resize(int x, int y, bool fromBottomRight); OnNodeUpdate() implements 8-handle resize with proportional-resize (aspect lock) logic.
- ShareX undo/redo: memento pattern via ImageEditorHistory — history.CreateShapesMemento() is called before mutating operations (add/delete/move); Ctrl+Z -> history.Undo(), Ctrl+Y -> history.Redo(); RestoreState(ImageEditorMemento) rebuilds shapes and canvas.
- ShareX editor keyboard shortcuts (form_KeyDown in ShapeManager): M select, R/NumPad1 rectangle, E/NumPad2 ellipse, F/NumPad3 freehand, L/NumPad4 line, A/NumPad5 arrow, B/NumPad8 blur, P/NumPad9 pixelate; Delete / Shift+Delete (delete all); Ctrl+D duplicate, Ctrl+V paste; Home/End/PageUp/PageDown for z-order; Ctrl+S save, Ctrl+C copy, Ctrl+U upload; arrow keys move with Shift-modified speed; Escape is three-stage (HandleEscape: cancel creation -> deselect -> switch to select tool).
- ShareX non-destructive blur/pixelate (BaseEffectShape.cs): effects are shapes, not bitmap edits — CacheEffect() crops the underlying bitmap region and applies the effect asynchronously into cachedEffect for live preview; OnDraw() shows the cached bitmap or a 'Processing' overlay (OnDrawOverlay); the source image is untouched until export.
- ShareX effect flattening at export (ShapeManager.RenderOutputImage): sets IsRenderingOutput = true, MoveAll(-offset), then for each EffectShape calls shape.OnDrawFinal(g, bmpOutput) — which crops shape-rect∩bitmap, runs abstract ApplyEffect(croppedImage), draws it back — then renders DrawingShapes via OnDraw(g), then restores positions; effect shapes are applied BEFORE drawing shapes so blur never blurs annotations.
- ShareX BlurEffectShape: ApplyEffect delegates to ImageHelpers.BoxBlur(bmp, BlurRadius); preview/final only rendered 'if (BlurRadius > 1)'; BlurRadius persisted via AnnotationOptions.BlurRadius in OnConfigLoad()/OnConfigSave(); overlay label shows Resources.Blur + $" [{BlurRadius}]".
- Greenshot license: GPL-3.0; C# on .NET Framework 4.8 (builds with .NET SDK 9.0.311 tooling, VS2022+); still active — v1.3.315 released March 20, 2026; 2,313 commits, 173 releases; editor code lives in src/Greenshot.Editor.
- Greenshot Surface (Greenshot.Editor/Drawing/Surface.cs): inherits WinForms Control, implements ISurface and INotifyPropertyChanged; elements stored in IDrawableContainerList _elements with a separate IDrawableContainerList selectedElements; painting in SurfacePaint() applies _zoomMatrix/_inverseZoomMatrix then delegates to _elements.Draw(graphics, clone, renderMode, imageClipRectangle).
- Greenshot undo/redo: Stack<IMemento> _undoStack and _redoStack; MakeUndoable(IMemento memento, bool allowMerge) merges consecutive edits via _undoStack.Peek().Merge(memento) when allowMerge is true (e.g. drag coalescing); _inUndoRedo flag prevents re-entrancy; concrete mementos include SurfaceBackgroundChangeMemento, AddElementMemento, DeleteElementMemento, AddElementsMemento, DeleteElementsMemento.
- Greenshot DrawableContainer: extends AbstractFieldHolderWithChildren, implements IDrawableContainer, is [Serializable] (used for clipboard/duplicate); geometry as int Left/Top/Width/Height exposed as Bounds (NativeRect); hit-test ClickableAt(int x, int y) inflates bounds by 5 pixels for click tolerance; abstract Draw(Graphics, RenderMode) implemented per shape; DrawContent() applies filters and selection border before Draw().
- Greenshot handles: IList<IAdorner> Adorners; CreateDefaultAdorners() adds eight ResizeAdorner objects at Positions.TopLeft, Positions.MiddleRight, etc.; FindActiveAdorner(MouseEventArgs) on the Surface routes mouse to handles; a special TargetAdorner (InitTargetAdorner) drives e.g. the speech-bubble pointer target.
- Greenshot editable-property system: every shape property (line color, thickness, blur radius...) is a typed Field on the container — AddField(GetType(), FieldType.X, default), GetFieldValueAsInt(FieldType), HandleFieldChanged events — which is what makes the toolbar auto-bind to the selected shape type.
- Greenshot non-destructive obscure: ObfuscateContainer : FilterContainer with AddField(GetType(), FieldType.PREPARED_FILTER_OBFUSCATE, PreparedFilter.PIXELIZE) (pixelize is default); ConfigurePreparedFilters() switches: case PreparedFilter.BLUR: Add(new BlurFilter(this)); case PreparedFilter.PIXELIZE: Add(new PixelizationFilter(this)); filters attach as IFilter children of the container (Filters property, HasFilters).
- Greenshot BlurFilter : AbstractFilter — signature 'public override void Apply(Graphics graphics, Bitmap applyBitmap, NativeRect rect, RenderMode renderMode)': at every paint it re-blurs the current background region under the container (fully non-destructive, filters compose with whatever is below); default AddField(..., FieldType.BLUR_RADIUS, 3); fast path GdiPlusApi.DrawWithBlur(graphics, applyBitmap, applyRect, null, null, blurRadius, false), fallback clones region into a fast bitmap and runs ImageHelper.ApplyBoxBlur(fastBitmap, blurRadius).
- Greenshot export pipeline: Surface.GetImageForExport() calls GetImage(RenderMode.EXPORT), which clones the original image and draws all elements onto it via _elements.Draw(...) — flatten happens only here; output then fans out to destination classes in src/Greenshot/Destinations: ClipboardDestination, EmailDestination, FileDestination, FileWithDialogDestination, PickerDestination, PrinterDestination, Win10OcrDestination, Win10ShareDestination (destination/plugin pattern).
- Greenshot region-select overlay (src/Greenshot/Forms/CaptureForm.cs): CreateParams uses WS_POPUP plus WS_EX_COMPOSITED ('prevent children to draw backgrounds'); bounds = _capture.ScreenBounds; OnPaint draws graphics.DrawImageUnscaled(_capture.Image, Point.Empty); rubber-band kept in _captureRect built as new NativeRect(...).Normalize(); selection tinted with graphics.FillRectangle(GreenOverlayBrush, fixedRect) plus measurement rulers.
- Greenshot window snapping: during Animate() it iterates a pre-populated _windows list using window.Contains(cursorPosition) and window.FindChildUnderPoint(cursorPosition) to snap selection to top-level windows and child controls; transitions animated by RectangleAnimator (_windowAnimator with FramesForMillis(700), _zoomAnimator); magnifier DrawZoom() clips to a GraphicsPath ellipse via graphics.SetClip(path), magnifying a 25x25 px source around the cursor with crosshair and ColorMatrix opacity.
- Flameshot license: GPLv3+ for the code, with logo under Free Art License v1.3, button icons under Apache 2.0, and some code derived from GPLv2 projects; C++ with Qt >= 6.2.4, CMake >= 3.22.
- Flameshot overlay (src/widgets/capture/capturewidget.cpp): frameless always-on-top window with platform-specific flags — Windows: Qt::WindowStaysOnTopHint | Qt::FramelessWindowHint | Qt::SubWindow; macOS: Qt::WindowStaysOnTopHint | Qt::FramelessWindowHint | Qt::Tool; Linux/Wayland: Qt::BypassWindowManagerHint | Qt::WindowStaysOnTopHint | Qt::FramelessWindowHint | Qt::Tool; background = ScreenGrabber::grabEntireDesktop() stored in m_context.screenshot; device-pixel-ratio adjustments applied on Windows.
- Flameshot region selection: a dedicated SelectionWidget* m_selection manages the rubber-band; normalized geometry stored in m_context.selection; its geometrySettled signal repositions the floating toolbar via m_buttonHandler->updatePosition().
- Flameshot tool object model: every annotation is a CaptureTool subclass; tool lifecycle = m_activeButton->tool()->copy(this) on mouse-press (startDrawObjectTool), drawMove()/drawMoveWithAdjustment() on drag, drawEnd() on release, then pushToolToStack() appends to m_captureToolObjects; rendering re-plays the stack — processPixmapWithTool() calls tool->process(painter, *pixmap) for each object over the pristine screenshot, which is what makes blur/pixelate non-destructive and reorderable.
- Flameshot tool base classes (src/tools/): capturetool.h (interface), abstracttwopointtool (line/arrow/rect/circle-style), abstractpathtool (pencil/marker), abstractactiontool (buttons like undo/copy/save), toolfactory.h/cpp (factory), capturecontext.h/cpp; tool subdirectories: accept, arrow, circle, circlecount, copy, exit, imgupload, invert, launcher, line, marker, move, pencil, pin, pixelate, rectangle, redo, save, selection, sizedecrease, sizeincrease, text, undo.
- Flameshot undo/redo: QUndoStack m_undoStack holding ModificationCommand objects that wrap whole-stack snapshots of m_captureToolObjects (backup taken via m_captureToolObjectsBackup before mutation, then pushObjectsStateToUndoStack()); undo()/redo() pop/push and trigger full re-render via drawToolsData().
- Flameshot pixelate security detail (src/tools/pixelate/pixelatetool.cpp): default mode is 'secure pseudo-pixelate' — it samples ONLY fringe pixels (four edge strips of the selection), synthesizes a low-res image by interpolating fringe colors with noise, and scales it up, so original content is unrecoverable; the optional useInsecurePixelate mode does classic downscale/upscale (scaled(effectSize, Qt::IgnoreAspectRatio, Qt::SmoothTransformation) then scaled back) or, for small areas, a QGraphicsBlurEffect with setBlurRadius(10) rendered twice.
- Flameshot export: on accept, pixmap() returns m_context.selectedScreenshotArea() (tool stack already composited), scaled by device pixel ratio and offset by m_context.widgetOffset, then handed to Flameshot::instance()->exportCapture() for clipboard/file/upload.
- kImageAnnotator (github.com/ksnip/kImageAnnotator): LGPL-3.0, C++/Qt5+Qt6 — the only purpose-built reusable annotation-canvas library found; used by ksnip and packaged in distros (also forked by KDE as KDE/libkimageannotator); consumed via CMake find_package(kImageAnnotator-Qt5 or -Qt6) + target_link_libraries(myApp kImageAnnotator); depends on the kColorPicker library; supports shared and static builds.
- kImageAnnotator architecture: AnnotationArea inherits QGraphicsScene (attachable to any QGraphicsView); a KImageAnnotation widget hosts the view plus toolbars; source layout src/annotations/{core, items, misc, modifiers, properties, undo} plus src/{backend, common, gui, widgets}.
- kImageAnnotator item hierarchy (src/annotations/items): abstract bases AbstractAnnotationItem, AbstractAnnotationLine, AbstractAnnotationRect, AbstractAnnotationPath, AbstractAnnotationPointerRect, AbstractAnnotationObfuscate, BaseAnnotationNumber; concrete items AnnotationRect, AnnotationEllipse, AnnotationLine, AnnotationArrow, AnnotationDoubleArrow, AnnotationPen, AnnotationMarkerPen/MarkerRect/MarkerEllipse, AnnotationText, AnnotationTextArrow, AnnotationTextPointer, AnnotationNumber, AnnotationNumberArrow, AnnotationNumberPointer, AnnotationBlur, AnnotationPixelate, AnnotationImage, AnnotationSticker, AnnotationDuplicate — note blur and pixelate are first-class scene items (AbstractAnnotationObfuscate), i.e. non-destructive by construction.
- kImageAnnotator undo (src/annotations/undo): QUndoStack-style UndoStack plus command classes AddCommand, DeleteCommand, MoveCommand, ResizeCommand, ScaleCommand, RotateCommand, FlipCommand, ArrangeCommand (z-order), ChangePropertiesCommand, CropCommand, CutCommand, PasteCommand, ModifyCanvasCommand — a per-operation command pattern (finer-grained than Flameshot's snapshot approach).
- swappy (github.com/jtheoof/swappy): MIT license — C (~93%), GTK GUI, cairo rendering, Wayland-native only (no X11/Windows); annotation modes: brush, text, rectangle, ellipse, arrow, blur; Meson build. MIT means its blur/tool-state-machine code may be copied into a proprietary app provided the copyright + license notice is retained.
- Text Grab (github.com/TheJoeFin/Text-Grab): MIT license, C#/WPF (99.8% C#, uses 'WPF UI - Fluent UI Style'), implements a fullscreen region-select overlay ('Full-Screen Mode' — select any region, OCR to clipboard; Escape/right-click to exit) — a directly copyable MIT reference for a WPF fullscreen capture overlay.
- ScreenToGif (github.com/NickeManarin/ScreenToGif): Microsoft Public License (MS-PL), C#/.NET/WPF screen recorder with a full integrated editor — MS-PL permits use in proprietary binaries (binary distribution under any compliant license) but source-form redistribution of its code must remain MS-PL, and MS-PL is GPL-incompatible; useful as a large WPF editor codebase to study or reuse under MS-PL terms.
- drweb86/annotator: screenshot annotation app in C# on .NET 10 + Avalonia UI + SkiaSharp (Windows and Ubuntu); features arrows, callouts, notes, blur, highlighter, 'cut out vertical and horizontal slices', OCR via Tesseract and Windows.Media.OCR; active (17 releases, latest June 13, 2026; 137 commits); LICENSE.md literally states 'Software is licensed under any OSI approved license up to your preference' — i.e. licensee may elect MIT, making it effectively permissive, though the unusual grant merits legal review before copying code.
- Core2D (github.com/wieslawsoltes/Core2D): MIT license — multi-platform data-driven 2D diagram editor in C# with built-in WYSIWYG vector editor, shape tools, layers, data binding to shapes, export to PDF/bitmap/DXF, C# scripting; the closest thing to a permissive .NET annotation-canvas codebase (shape model, selection/handles, undo) to mine for a proprietary WPF/Avalonia app. Sibling project Draw2D (also MIT, C#, Avalonia) was archived read-only on January 31, 2026.
- Microsoft PowerToys is MIT-licensed C# and contains production-quality fullscreen overlay utilities (Text Extractor — derived from Text Grab — and Screen Ruler) that are permissive references for Win32 overlay windows, per-monitor DPI, and capture UX on Windows.
- No mature MIT/Apache-licensed drop-in annotation-canvas library exists for .NET/WPF; practical permissive building blocks are SkiaSharp (MIT) or WPF's built-in DrawingVisual/InkCanvas plus the WPF Adorner framework (which natively mirrors Greenshot's IAdorner resize-gripper concept), with Core2D/Text Grab/ScreenToGif as copyable reference code.
- License wall for a proprietary clone: architecture, algorithms, UX flows, shortcut schemes, and data-model ideas gleaned from ShareX/Greenshot/Flameshot are NOT protected by copyright (ideas vs expression); what IS prohibited without GPL compliance: copying source, porting/translating GPL code to another language (a translation is a derivative work), or close paraphrase; linking any GPL library into the proprietary binary also triggers copyleft. For close functional clones the conservative practice is clean-room: one person reads GPL source and writes a functional spec, a different person implements from the spec.
- LGPL-3.0 (kImageAnnotator) is usable from a proprietary app only via dynamic linking with LGPL §4 compliance (ship library source/offer, permit user modification and relinking); copying its code into the app triggers full copyleft — for a WPF app it is irrelevant as a dependency (Qt) and safe only as a design reference.
- Common architecture pattern across all four codebases (safe to adopt as an idea): keep the pristine screenshot immutable; represent every annotation INCLUDING blur/pixelate as a vector/effect object in an ordered list; hit-test back-to-front; 8-9 resize handles owned by the object; re-render effects against the live background each frame (Greenshot filters, ShareX cached effect shapes, Flameshot/kImageAnnotator re-played tool stacks); undo = either whole-state mementos (ShareX, Flameshot) or per-operation commands (Greenshot merge-able mementos, kImageAnnotator QUndoStack commands); flatten effects-then-shapes only in the export path (RenderOutputImage / GetImageForExport / exportCapture).

## Recommendations
- Model the annotation object model on Greenshot's design (Surface + DrawableContainer + attachable IFilter children + typed Field system + merge-able memento stacks) — it maps most naturally onto WPF (Control->FrameworkElement, IAdorner->WPF Adorners, Fields->DependencyProperties) — but implement clean-room; do not port GPL code line-by-line.
- Implement obscure tools non-destructively as effect objects that re-sample the pristine background each render (Greenshot BlurFilter.Apply pattern or ShareX CacheEffect async-preview pattern); apply effect objects BEFORE vector shapes at export so blur never smears annotations (ShareX RenderOutputImage ordering).
- Adopt Flameshot's secure-pixelate insight: naive downscale/upscale pixelation and weak Gaussian blur are partially reversible; offer a mode that synthesizes the obscured region from fringe pixels + noise, and always compute obscuring from the full-resolution source.
- For copyable code, mine MIT sources only: Text Grab (WPF fullscreen overlay + OCR region select), Core2D (C# shape/handle/undo editor), swappy (minimal tool state machine + cairo blur), PowerToys overlays; treat ShareX/Greenshot/Flameshot strictly as read-only architecture references and kImageAnnotator as an LGPL design reference.
- Use whole-document snapshot undo (ShareX/Flameshot style, trivially correct) for v1, then migrate to per-operation commands with drag-merge (Greenshot MakeUndoable(allowMerge)/kImageAnnotator command set: Add/Delete/Move/Resize/Arrange/ChangeProperties/Crop/Scale) once the object model stabilizes.

# TOPIC: gap-5

## Summary
For the PDF export path, the build plan should specify one of two MIT-licensed options depending on the rendering backend: if the clone renders via SkiaSharp, use SkiaSharp's built-in SKDocument.CreatePdf (zero extra dependency, pages sized in points at 1pt = 1/72in, lossless image embedding by default at EncodingQuality 101); if the clone renders via WPF (RenderTargetBitmap), use PDFsharp 6.2.4 (MIT) with the PDFsharp-wpf package, whose XImage.FromBitmapSource accepts a BitmapSource directly and whose XGraphics.DrawImage takes an explicit rect in points (compute as pixels * 72 / DPI). QuestPDF is legal for a proprietary paid exe only while annual gross revenue stays under USD 1,000,000 (Community license; 90-day transition to a $1,999/yr Professional license if exceeded) and is layout-engine overkill for a single-bitmap page — it also depends on SkiaSharp internally. iText is AGPLv3 and confirmed disqualified for closed-source distribution without a commercial license. Key DPI gotcha for both MIT options: PDF has no DPI — physical size comes entirely from the destination rectangle in points, so always compute pageWidthPts = pixelWidth * 72 / imageDpi and draw the bitmap into exactly that rect rather than relying on default image sizing (PDFsharp's no-size DrawImage overload uses the image's embedded resolution metadata, so a 96-DPI screen bitmap comes out at 0.75pt per pixel).

## Facts
- PDFsharp is MIT-licensed: the empira/PDFsharp GitHub repo states 'Published Open Source under the MIT License' (github.com/empira/PDFsharp).
- PDFsharp current stable version is 6.2.4 (released January 2026); a 7.0.0 Preview 1 was published March 24, 2026 targeting C# 12 and .NET 8/9/10 (docs.pdfsharp.net + GitHub releases).
- PDFsharp 6.x supports .NET 8/9/10 plus .NET Framework 4.6.2 and .NET Standard 2.0 (docs.pdfsharp.net).
- PDFsharp coordinates are in points at 72 points per inch; 'PDFsharp is a .NET library for processing PDF files. You create PDF pages using drawing routines known from GDI+' (docs.pdfsharp.net).
- PDFsharp ships three builds: Core build natively supports only JPEG, PNG, and Windows BMP for image import; the GDI+ and WPF builds 'rely on Windows libraries to read images' and additionally support GIF and TIFF (docs.pdfsharp.net/PDFsharp/Topics/Bitmap-Images/Formats.html).
- PDFsharp Core build PNG support covers 1/4/8/24/32 BPP including 'True Color with or without transparency'; JPEG support 'Supports JFIF and EXIF and some other variations of .JPG files, but some variations of .JPG files may not work' (docs.pdfsharp.net Bitmap Formats page).
- PDFsharp's WPF build (NuGet package 'PDFsharp-wpf') provides XImage.FromBitmapSource(BitmapSource), converting a WPF BitmapSource (e.g., RenderTargetBitmap output) directly to an XImage for embedding — the natural fit if the clone renders annotations with WPF DrawingVisual/RenderTargetBitmap (pdfsharp.net NuGetPackage_PDFsharp-wpf page + forum.pdfsharp.net t=1118).
- PDFsharp image sizing formula used in the official empira samples: width-in-points = image.PixelWidth * 72 / image.HorizontalResolution (same for height) — i.e., DrawImage with an explicit XRect is how you hit exact physical dimensions (github.com/empira/PDFsharp-samples-1.5 Images.cs).
- PDFsharp gotcha: XImage exposes PointWidth/PointHeight derived from the image's embedded resolution metadata, so a typical 96-DPI Windows bitmap drawn without an explicit size renders at 0.75 pt per pixel; the build plan should always pass an explicit destination XRect computed from desired inches * 72 (docs.pdfsharp.net Accessibility page examples + empira samples).
- QuestPDF Community license revenue threshold (verbatim from questpdf.com/license/community.html, July 2026): free for 'individuals and businesses with annual gross revenue under USD 1,000,000'; commercial closed-source use is permitted under that threshold and redistribution is allowed 'only as a compiled component of your own Derivative Applications'.

- QuestPDF paid tiers as of July 2026 (questpdf.com/pricing.html): Professional '$1,999+ local tax' per year, Enterprise '$4,999+ local tax' per year; 'Every paid QuestPDF license covers an unlimited number of developers' — note these prices are higher than the historical $699/$1,999 figures.
- QuestPDF depends on SkiaSharp internally for rendering, so choosing it does not avoid the Skia native dependency — it adds a licensing condition on top of a library (SkiaSharp) the project could use directly under MIT.
- SkiaSharp is MIT-licensed (LICENSE.md/LICENSE.txt in mono/SkiaSharp show 'MIT, MIT licenses found'); latest release is 4.150.0, released July 9, 2026 (github.com/mono/SkiaSharp).
- SkiaSharp SKDocument.CreatePdf has 9 overloads: CreatePdf(string), CreatePdf(Stream), CreatePdf(SKWStream), each also available with a float dpi parameter or an SKDocumentPdfMetadata parameter (learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/skiasharp.skdocument.createpdf).
- SkiaSharp SKDocument.CreatePdf remarks (verbatim): 'PDF pages are sized in point units. 1 pt == 1/72 inch == 127/360 mm.' — so SKDocument.BeginPage(width, height) takes points, and physical size is pixelWidth / imageDpi * 72.
- SkiaSharp CreatePdf's dpi parameter (verbatim): 'The DPI (pixels-per-inch) at which features without native PDF support will be rasterized' — it does NOT affect the physical page size, only fallback rasterization of unsupported drawing features; SKDocumentPdfMetadata.DefaultRasterDpi is 72.0 DPI (learn.microsoft.com SKDocumentPdfMetadata docs).
- SkiaSharp SKDocumentPdfMetadata.EncodingQuality remarks (verbatim): 'Encoding quality controls the trade-off between size and quality. By default this is set to 101 percent, which corresponds to lossless encoding. If this value is set to a value <= 100, and the image is opaque, it will be encoded (using JPEG) with that quality setting.' DefaultEncodingQuality is '101% or lossless' — so a screenshot bitmap embeds losslessly by default; set <=100 to shrink file size via JPEG re-encode.
- SkiaSharp SKDocumentPdfMetadata also has a PdfA property ('whether to make the document PDF/A-2b conformant') plus Title/Author/Subject/Keywords/Creator/Producer/Creation/Modified metadata fields (learn.microsoft.com SKDocumentPdfMetadata docs).
- SkiaSharp bitmap-in-PDF pattern: using var doc = SKDocument.CreatePdf(stream, metadata); var canvas = doc.BeginPage(wPts, hPts); canvas.DrawImage(skImage, SKRect.Create(0, 0, wPts, hPts)); doc.EndPage(); doc.Close(); — the destination SKRect in points is what fixes physical dimensions/DPI.
- iText is AGPLv3 and effectively disqualified: iText's own licensing page states 'You may not deploy it on a network without disclosing the full source code of your own applications under the AGPL license', that using it 'in a non-AGPL environment' is 'a legal violation', that you must 'retain the producer line in every PDF that is created', and 'If you cannot comply with the AGPLv3 terms, we have various commercial licensing options which will release you of these restrictions' (itextpdf.com/how-buy/agpl-license).
- Recommendation for the build plan: if SkiaSharp is already the rendering backend, specify SKDocument.CreatePdf (zero added dependency, MIT, lossless-by-default embedding, point-based page sizing); if the renderer is pure WPF, specify PDFsharp 6.2.4 via the PDFsharp-wpf package (MIT, XImage.FromBitmapSource, explicit XRect sizing at pixels*72/DPI); QuestPDF only if a document layout engine is wanted and the sub-$1M revenue cap is accepted as a business risk; iText excluded on AGPL grounds.
- DPI-fidelity rule common to both MIT options: PDF is a vector format with no intrinsic DPI — 'PDFsharp uses Points as the unit for coordinates, with 72 Points per Inch' — so export code must derive page size in points from the capture's pixel dimensions and the intended DPI (e.g., a 1920x1080 capture at 144 DPI target -> 960x540 pt page), never from the bitmap's default 96-DPI metadata.

## Recommendations
- Specify SkiaSharp SKDocument.CreatePdf as the primary PDF export path if the clone's rendering/export pipeline is already SkiaSharp-based: MIT license, no new dependency, and lossless bitmap embedding by default (EncodingQuality 101).
- Specify PDFsharp 6.2.4 (PDFsharp-wpf NuGet package, MIT) as the PDF writer if the clone renders with WPF RenderTargetBitmap — XImage.FromBitmapSource avoids a re-encode round-trip through PNG.
- In either case, compute the PDF page size in points explicitly (points = pixels * 72 / exportDpi) and draw the bitmap into a destination rect of exactly that size; never rely on default image-resolution-based sizing (96-DPI metadata shrinks output to 0.75 pt/pixel in PDFsharp).

# TOPIC: gap-6

## Summary
Package identity is NOT required to invoke the Windows Share sheet as a share SOURCE from a fully unpackaged .NET exe. Microsoft's current (June 2026) share documentation explicitly states desktop apps "whether packaged or unpackaged" use IDataTransferManagerInterop.GetForWindow + ShowShareUIForWindow, and Microsoft ships an unpackaged WPF ShareSource sample. Chrome and Firefox — both unpackaged Win32 installs on hundreds of millions of machines — use exactly this API in production on Win10/Win11. Identity (sparse MSIX / "package with external location") is only required to RECEIVE shares (register as a Share Target) — per the sample README, "Win32 is not supported for Sharing content target apps" without identity. The main practical caveat for iSkitch-clone is image payloads: SetBitmap-only shares surface far fewer targets (e.g., Mail/OneNote but not Facebook/Instagram) because most targets register for image FILES, so the share flow should write a temp PNG and call SetStorageItems + SetBitmap together. Known reliability wrinkles: the DataRequested handler must complete without throwing (Chromium always returns S_OK because a FAILED return gets the process killed by the OS), the DataTransferManager returned by GetForWindow must be kept rooted in a field, the call must happen on the UI thread owning the HWND, and Chromium adds a watchdog timeout because DataRequested occasionally never fires. There is one open WinUI 3-specific bug (share UI auto-dismisses after ~1s on Win10 21H2, microsoft-ui-xaml #8859) not reproduced from plain WPF/WinForms. Verdict for the build plan: include the native Share button; no sparse package needed unless the app later wants to be a share target or send toasts.

## Facts
- Microsoft's 'Share content from your app' doc (learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/windows-integration/integrate-sharesheet-send, ms.date 2026-06-22) states verbatim: 'Desktop apps - whether packaged or unpackaged - use the IDataTransferManagerInterop interface to access the Share Sheet on a per-window basis. This applies to WinUI 3, WPF, and WinForms apps.' — i.e., no package identity required to be a share source.
- The same doc's approach table lists: 'Desktop apps (WinUI 3, WPF, WinForms) | Use IDataTransferManagerInterop for per-window sharing (packaged or unpackaged) | Windows Runtime via COM interop'.
- The same doc warns: 'DataTransferManager.GetForCurrentView and ShowShareUI are supported only in UWP apps. Desktop apps (WinUI 3, WPF, or WinForms - packaged or unpackaged) must use the IDataTransferManagerInterop pattern.' GetForCurrentView from WinUI3/desktop throws COMException 'Element not found' (microsoft-ui-xaml issue #4886).
- Exact C# interop recipe from Microsoft docs: declare IDataTransferManagerInterop via [ComImport] Guid("3A3DCD6C-3EAB-43DC-BCDE-45671CE800C8") InterfaceType(InterfaceIsIUnknown) with methods 'IntPtr GetForWindow([In] IntPtr appWindow, [In] ref Guid riid)' and 'void ShowShareUIForWindow(IntPtr appWindow)'; DataTransferManager IID riid = a5caee9b-8708-49d1-8d36-67d25a8da00c; acquire via DataTransferManager.As<IDataTransferManagerInterop>() then WinRT.MarshalInterface<DataTransferManager>.FromAbi(interop.GetForWindow(hWnd, _dtm_iid)); subscribe DataRequested; call interop.ShowShareUIForWindow(hWnd).
- The plain 'as DataTransferManager' cast on GetForWindow's return is always null in C#/CsWinRT (microsoft/CsWinRT issue #970) — you MUST use WinRT.MarshalInterface<DataTransferManager>.FromAbi(...); this was the failure mode developers hit, not an identity problem.
- Microsoft's official docs sample stores the DataTransferManager in an instance field (_dtm) after GetForWindow — keep it rooted so the DataRequested handler isn't garbage-collected between button clicks.
- HWND acquisition per framework (from the June 2026 doc): WPF 'new System.Windows.Interop.WindowInteropHelper(this).Handle'; WinForms 'this.Handle'; WinUI 3 'WinRT.Interop.WindowNative.GetWindowHandle(this)'.
- Microsoft ships an official UNPACKAGED WPF share-source sample: microsoft/Windows-classic-samples/Samples/ShareSource (wpf/DataTransferManagerHelper.cs), 'Shows how a managed Win32 app can participate in the Share contract', sharing Text, Web link, and Files; requires only 'Windows 10 to execute' — no packaging step in the sample.
- The ShareSource sample README states verbatim: 'Win32 is not supported for Sharing content target apps' — Win32/unpackaged apps can be share SOURCES only; being a share TARGET is what requires identity.
- The 'Integrate unpackaged apps with Windows Share' doc (integrate-sharesheet-unpackaged.md in MicrosoftDocs/windows-dev-docs) says identity is needed only for receiving: 'An unpackaged app must be provided with package identity so it can register as a Share Target.' It covers zero requirements for the send/source side.
- Real-world production proof #1: Chromium (unpackaged Win32) implements Web Share / sharing hub on Windows via chrome/browser/webshare/win/share_operation.cc and show_share_ui_for_window_operation.cc, using IDataTransferManagerInterop GetForWindow + ShowShareUIForWindow, shipping in Chrome on Win10/Win11.
- Real-world production proof #2: Firefox (unpackaged when installed via its regular installer) implements the Windows share dialog in widget/windows/WindowsUIUtils.cpp using RoGetActivationFactory(RuntimeClass_Windows_ApplicationModel_DataTransfer_DataTransferManager, IID_PPV_ARGS(&dtmInterop)), dtmInterop->GetForWindow(hwnd, ...), then ShowShareUIForWindow(hwnd), with ShareCompleted/ShareCanceled listeners via IDataPackage3/IDataPackage4 (tracked in Bugzilla bug 1363169 'Add support for native Windows Share feature').
- Chromium reliability hardening #1 (show_share_ui_for_window_operation.cc): the DataRequested callback must 'Always return S_OK, as returning a FAILED value results in the OS killing this process. If the data population failed the OS Share operation will fail gracefully with messaging to the user.' — wrap your C# DataRequested handler in try/catch.
- Chromium reliability hardening #2: Chromium schedules a kMaxExecutionTime timeout around ShowShareUIForWindow because the DataRequested event can fail to arrive: 'If the call is expected to complete later, save the clean-up callback for later use and schedule a timeout to cover any cases where it fails (and therefore never comes)' — i.e., occasional silent no-show is a known OS-level flake Chromium defends against.
- Chromium enforces UI-thread execution for all share-sheet interop (DCHECK_CURRENTLY_ON(content::BrowserThread::UI)) — call GetForWindow/ShowShareUIForWindow on the thread that owns the HWND (WPF dispatcher thread).
- SetBitmap-only shares reduce available targets: Microsoft Q&A 'Using SetBitmap with the DataPackage does not work for some apps' (learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/389/) — sharing only a bitmap surfaced only Mail and OneNote; Facebook and Instagram were absent. Accepted answer: 'Only apps which support receiving Bitmaps will show up if you share only a Bitmap. It is much more common for apps to receive image files than uncompressed Bitmaps.'
- Recommended image-share pattern (Microsoft Q&A + microsoft/Windows-universal-samples ShareSource ShareImage.xaml.cs): set BOTH request.Data.SetBitmap(RandomAccessStreamReference.CreateFromFile(file)) AND request.Data.SetStorageItems(new[]{ file }) — i.e., for iSkitch-clone, flatten the annotated image to a temp .png, StorageFile.GetFileFromPathAsync(tempPath), then SetStorageItems + SetBitmap; StorageFile.GetFileFromPathAsync works in full-trust unpackaged apps.
- DataPackage best practices from the June 2026 doc: Properties.Title is required; use SetWebLink/SetApplicationLink (not SetText) for URLs; source-side events available to desktop apps via the GetForWindow-acquired manager: DataTransferManager.TargetApplicationChosen, DataPackage.ShareCompleted, DataPackage.ShareCanceled; DataPackage.OperationCompleted/Destroyed are for clipboard, not share.
- If sharing files asynchronously, request a deferral in DataRequested (args.Request.GetDeferral()) — Chromium uses this pattern with CreateStreamedFileAsync for large payloads; for a small annotated PNG a synchronous temp-file + SetStorageItems is sufficient.
- API availability: IDataTransferManagerInterop::ShowShareUIForWindow is in shobjidl_core.h (include Shobjidl.h); the SDK requirements table says 'Minimum supported client: Windows 8 [UWP apps only]' (the '[UWP apps only]' tag is a doc artifact — the interop interface exists precisely for non-UWP callers and works on all Windows 10/11 builds; Win10 1507+ in practice, so Win10 22H2 and Win11 23H2/24H2 are all covered).
- Known open bug (WinUI 3-specific, not reproduced from plain WPF/WinForms): microsoft/microsoft-ui-xaml issue #8859 'Interop Datatransfermanager shown for a short time, almost 1 second Win10 21H2' — share UI opens then auto-dismisses ~1s later in a WinUI3 app on Win10 build 19044, Windows App SDK 1.3; still open, no root cause, no workaround documented. Same symptom appeared during Firefox's development (bug 1363169: dialog 'would show a loading screen then disappear') and was resolved there — commonly linked to the DataRequested handler erroring/completing without data.
- microsoft/WindowsAppSDK issue #2174 'Share UI gets Crashed' (WinAppSDK 1.0, packaged MSIX, Win10 21H1): share UI failed to appear on some systems with no error, worked on others; closed 'Resolution-No-Repro' — evidence that share-sheet no-shows are environmental/OS-side and affect packaged apps too (packaging is not the differentiator).
- Elevated processes are a risk zone: WinUI/WinRT shell-integration surfaces generally fail when run as administrator (e.g., FileOpenPicker crashes elevated apps, microsoft/WindowsAppSDK issue #2504; WinUI 3 'does not support running elevated due to a limitation in WinRT'). No authoritative doc found for ShowShareUIForWindow specifically, but do not offer Share from an elevated instance without testing.
- Environmental note for Win11 24H2/25H2 test matrix: a July 2025 Patch Tuesday update (KB5062553) broke XAML-dependent shell surfaces (Start menu, Search, SystemSettings, Taskbar, Explorer) on some 24H2/25H2 PCs; Microsoft says it 'is addressed starting with the June 23, 2026 Windows update, KB5095093' — the Win11 share sheet is a shell-hosted XAML surface, so share-sheet flakiness on un-patched 24H2 machines can be OS breakage, not the app's packaging.
- Fallback spec if identity is ever wanted (only needed for share TARGET, toasts, etc.) — 'package with external location' (sparse package), from learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/desktop/modernize/grant-identity-to-nonpackaged-apps (ms.date 2026-04-08): requires Windows 10 build 10.0.19041.0+ (uap10:AllowExternalContent introduced there; doc recommends TargetDeviceFamily MinVersion=10.0.19041.0, MaxVersionTested=10.0.26100.0).

- Sparse-package build/deploy: 'MakeAppx.exe pack /o /d <dir> /nv /p MyPackage.msix' (the /nv flag is required to bypass validation of referenced file paths); must be signed (SignTool, cert Subject must equal Identity Publisher); self-signed dev certs must be imported to Trusted People or registration fails with CERT_E_UNTRUSTEDROOT 0x800B0109; register per-user via 'Add-AppxPackage -Path <pkg> -ExternalLocation <installDir>' or PackageManager.AddPackageByUriAsync with AddPackageOptions.ExternalLocationUri; common errors: 0x80073CF9 version already registered, 0x80073D54 manifest metadata mismatch, 0x80073CF6 manifest invalid.
- Sparse-package cost summary for the build plan: code-signing certificate is mandatory (production cert e.g. Azure Trusted Signing for end-user machines), installer must register/unregister the identity package — this entire cost is avoidable for iSkitch-clone's share-SOURCE feature, which needs none of it.

## Recommendations
- Include the native Share button in the build plan for the unpackaged exe — no sparse MSIX needed. Use the documented pattern: ComImport IDataTransferManagerInterop (GUID 3A3DCD6C-3EAB-43DC-BCDE-45671CE800C8), GetForWindow(hwnd, a5caee9b-8708-49d1-8d36-67d25a8da00c), WinRT.MarshalInterface<DataTransferManager>.FromAbi, store the manager in a field, subscribe DataRequested once, then ShowShareUIForWindow(hwnd) on the WPF dispatcher thread.

- Harden like Chromium: wrap the DataRequested handler body in try/catch so it always completes cleanly (an exception escaping the handler can get the process killed by the OS), and treat a share sheet that never calls back as a non-fatal outcome — keep copy-to-clipboard and save-as as always-available siblings of Share.
